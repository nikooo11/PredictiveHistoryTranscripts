# His Big Ideas: The Concepts Behind the Characters

*Draws mainly on Geo-Strategy END, Civilization BONUS and #END, Secret History #1–3, 7–9, 13, 21 and END, Geo-Strategy Update #3–5, Game Theory #1, 5, 10–14, 17–19, 22, 24, 26, 28–29, Great Books #1 and #13, the 2023 Gay Talese video and the 2026 meet-ups. Other videos were searched but not read in full.*

## How he thinks

Jiang calls his project **predictive history**. In June 2025 he said history should "connect the events of the past into a coherent story", explain the present and predict the future. If it does all three, he said, "it must be true history" [Civ BONUS · 04:13](https://youtu.be/voQEteh6Hko?t=253) [Civ BONUS · 04:58](https://youtu.be/voQEteh6Hko?t=298). Behind the project sits a boyhood dream taken from Isaac Asimov: psychohistory, "the idea that the future can be predicted and if it can be predicted then it can be controlled" [GS END · 02:59](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=179).

He works the same loop in every series. He builds an "analytical model" of current events and uses it to make predictions, which are the test of the model [SH 1 · 05:04](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=304). "It's no different from artificial intelligence," he says [SH 1 · 05:27](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=327). A model that predicts well is then turned on the past "to reveal the secret history of the world". In his telling, the history taught in school "is false", "a system implanted into your brains by powerful people" [SH 1 · 05:43](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=343) [SH 1 · 06:07](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=367).

His main tool is his own version of **game theory**. Every situation has players, rules and incentives, and once you understand all three, "therefore you can predict how the game will turn out" [GT 1 · 06:13](https://youtu.be/hE4l9WyLF3U?t=373). It is deliberately amoral: "there's really no good and evil" [GS END · 26:04](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=1564). It is also not the academic kind. He calls the Nash equilibrium "just a theory we made up" [GT 1 · 17:36](https://youtu.be/hE4l9WyLF3U?t=1056). On this base he builds named laws: a universal law (mass × energy × coordination), plus laws of asymmetry, escalation, eschatological convergence and proximity (see the Glossary).

His second tool is **stories**. Groups coordinate best around a shared story, which acts as "a script" [GSU 5 · 23:02](https://youtu.be/5I2VPYPJJ68?t=1382), or "the operating system of a society" [GT 12 · 18:41](https://youtu.be/spg58Glfz68?t=1121). The strongest kind of story is an **eschatology**, a story of how the world ends. By May 2026 he was arguing that eschatology "is really just an understanding of geopolitics framed allegorically" [GT 28 · 11:13](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=673). So a war that "makes no sense" in realpolitik terms makes "perfect sense" once you read the religious script behind it [GT 12 · 1:00:14](https://youtu.be/spg58Glfz68?t=3614).

His third tool is **imagination**. From Kant he takes the idea that "reality is what we imagine it to be" [SH 1 · 02:49](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=169). Money, the individual and the nation-state are, for him, shared acts of imagination that power keeps alive [SH 1 · 1:09:24](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=4164). The same idea carries his message of hope. Citing Dante, he tells students that "the future is what you imagine and fight for" [GS END · 01:03](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=63).

In his telling, history is driven by **elites** and the fictions they tell. Mass society needs an "artificial Elite", which must "create fictions" to justify itself [GS END · 37:54](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=2274). Societies decay when elites multiply, turn to rent-seeking and split into warring factions [SH 2 · 38:05](https://youtu.be/K-_l9jBGo74?t=2285). In 2026 the account became conspiratorial: he said occult-minded secret societies choose "agents or puppets", from Napoleon and Hitler to Trump and Putin, to act out their eschatology [GT 28 · 28:28](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=1708). He offers no evidence for this (see *Occult and secret societies*).

Two habits hold the method together. First, he asks to be judged by results. "If I miss one then all my theory is wrong," he says of his predictions [GT 11 · 05:02](https://youtu.be/fz-Dan7NRss?t=302), and he is "always looking to invalidate my theory" [GT 29 · 1:11:11](https://youtu.be/RE2UribEFIo?t=4271). Second, he labels much of it speculation. He introduced one finance lecture as "fun topics that have no scholarly basis" [GT 17 · 02:33](https://youtu.be/VbowBTJWKBo?t=153).

The vocabulary built up in layers:

- **June 2024:** psychohistory and modelling with AI.
- **June 2025:** "predictive history" gets its name.
- **July 2025:** the universal law of game theory.
- **August to October 2025:** secret history and the "death by…" lectures.
- **March 2026:** the four named laws.
- **May 2026:** the combined account of the occult and AI.

## Influences he names

### Isaac Asimov and Hari Seldon

*Who:* Isaac Asimov (1920–1992) was an American science-fiction writer and biochemist. In his *Foundation* series (stories from 1942, collected as novels in 1951–53), the mathematician Hari Seldon invents "psychohistory", a statistical science of mass behaviour. Seldon foresees the fall of a Galactic Empire and sets up the Foundation to shorten the dark age that follows.

**What he teaches**
- **Where it started.** As a boy in Toronto who "didn't have that many friends", he loved the *Foundation* books. His long-term goal is to "build intellectual foundations for the possibility of psycho history". He also wants to found a school "like Plato's academy", even "the Jedi temple" [Civ BONUS · 05:27](https://youtu.be/voQEteh6Hko?t=327) [Civ BONUS · 10:49](https://youtu.be/voQEteh6Hko?t=649).
- **The premise, as he retells it.** Asimov's galactic empire must collapse into "30,000 years of war violence barbarity". Psychohistory maps human behaviour to find its patterns. Once you have them, "you can manipulate the course of events to make a better future" [GS END · 04:00](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=240).
- **Building it with AI (2024).** The recipe has four steps:
  1. Turn a prediction, such as a US war on Iran, into a model.
  2. Refine the model against "hundreds of wars", from Athens's invasion of Sicily to Operation Barbarossa.
  3. Combine many such models until "you develop psycho history".
  4. Allow "50 to 100 years" for the whole project.

  [GS END · 24:58](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=1498) [GS END · 26:04](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=1564) [GS END · 43:00](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=2580)
- **The edge-case problem.** Great men "step outside of history", so no model can foresee them. His examples are Homer, Dante, Jesus, Caesar and Putin. Asimov's fix was the Second Foundation, a hidden team of telepaths who correct the model. Jiang takes the idea further: perhaps great men are themselves telepaths. He calls Putin "almost a telepath" [GS END · 48:29](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=2909) [GS END · 49:32](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=2972) [GS END · 52:14](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=3134).
- **Who should control it.** A "second Foundation" of scholars should run the AI. It must stay "open and transparent" and democratic, or the elite will abuse it [GS END · 1:01:30](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=3690).
- **Second thoughts (2026).** He now notes that "tech oligarchs obsess over" *Foundation*, with Peter Thiel and Elon Musk hoping to shorten the coming dark age [Meet-Up 2 · 23:10](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=1390) [Meet-Up 2 · 23:28](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=1408). He calls Musk's fandom "a very telling comment" [Emergency 2 · 2:15:40](https://youtu.be/Z1G6WI1Q0M8?t=8140). In Austin he said these men read the books as kids, "like myself". He once accepted their logic of destroying the world in order to repair it faster, but "Now I disagree" [Meet-Up 4 · 2:21:23](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=8483) [Meet-Up 4 · 2:22:03](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=8523).

**In his big picture:** Psychohistory is the goal the whole channel aims at. Predictive history is his hand-made version of it, with game theory and eschatology standing in for Seldon's equations. The edge-case problem is where his "great men" enter: Hegel's world-historical figure, Nietzsche's Übermensch, and Putin.

**In his words:** "psycho history is the idea that the future can be predicted and if it can be predicted then it can be controlled and harnessed for the betterment of humanity" [GS END · 02:59](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=179)

**Reality check:** Psychohistory is fiction. In the novels it works only for vast populations and cannot foresee individuals, which is why the mutant "Mule" wrecks Seldon's plan. Real quantitative history, such as Turchin's cliodynamics, is an active but contested field, and no model has shown anything like the predictive reach Jiang describes. Jiang never names Seldon in the transcripts; an audience member does [Meet-Up 4 · 2:20:53](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=8453).

### Gay Talese

*Who:* Gay Talese (born 1932) is an American journalist and author, and a pioneer of the literary "New Journalism" of the 1960s. He is known for the *Esquire* profile "Frank Sinatra Has a Cold" (1966) and for the books *The Kingdom and the Power* (1969), *Honor Thy Father* (1971) and *Thy Neighbor's Wife* (1981).

**What he teaches**
- **His mentor.** In 1999 Talese came to Beijing to work on a story. Jiang, just out of Yale and teaching there, became his translator for six months. He calls it "the best education any young writer could receive" [GB 13 · 00:12](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=12) [GB 13 · 00:34](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=34) [GB 13 · 00:54](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=54).
- **The research method (a Beijing class, May 2023).** The method has two halves, each learned from one parent in the family tailoring business:
  - **Exploration** comes from Talese's mother, who won over demanding rich clients simply by listening to them.
  - **Reflection** comes from his father, the patient, precise tailor.

  In Jiang's words, "exploration is the pursuit of curiosity and reflection is a pursuit of perfection" [Talese 2023 · 02:01](https://youtu.be/Gp9Hv1PXx0o?t=121) [Talese 2023 · 03:03](https://youtu.be/Gp9Hv1PXx0o?t=183).
- **Literary journalism.** This is the idea that journalism "can be by itself be literature". It teaches "how to be creative how to be imaginative and how to engage with reality", which he says schools discourage [Talese 2023 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/Gp9Hv1PXx0o?t=0).
- **How Talese works.** Talese spends "hours and hours", then "days, months", with a subject and the people around them. He is trying "to draw out a memory" that will resonate with readers. Jiang says Talese's research boxes "are living souls" [GB 13 · 00:54](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=54) [GB 13 · 01:43](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=103) [GB 13 · 04:00](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=240).
- ***Thy Neighbor's Wife* as scripture.** In his last lecture at the Beijing school (May 2026), he read Talese's book about sex as a religious quest. Talese "needed to go into the heart of darkness itself", and he came out as someone who creates "sparks of light". For Jiang, creating those sparks is the purpose of life [GB 13 · 06:47](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=407) [GB 13 · 45:13](https://youtu.be/VWmPHHnZuiw?t=2713).

**In his big picture:** Talese is the human, literary half of his method. Patient listening and exact craft balance the modelling half. In Jiang's cosmology, writing that draws out memorable "sparks" is imagination at work, and imagination is the reason humans exist.

**In his words:** "exploration is the pursuit of curiosity and reflection is a pursuit of perfection" [Talese 2023 · 03:03](https://youtu.be/Gp9Hv1PXx0o?t=183)

**Reality check:** *Thy Neighbor's Wife* is usually read as reportage on America's sexual revolution. The Kabbalistic, religious reading is Jiang's own.

### Ibn Khaldun

*Who:* Ibn Khaldun (1332–1406) was a North African Arab historian. His *Muqaddimah* explains the rise and fall of dynasties through *asabiyyah*, or group solidarity. It is strongest among hardy frontier peoples and wears away over a few generations of settled luxury.

**What he teaches**
- **A new theory of empire.** He presents Ibn Khaldun (captioned "Ibin Kudan") as offering "a new way to understand how empires come into being". The key word, asabiyyah, means "cohesion or group solidarity" [GT 5 · 02:48](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=168) [GT 5 · 03:04](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=184).
- **The margins beat the centre.** Poor peoples on the margins are "more unified". Rich centres grow "too individualistic" and "too corrupt". So frontier tribes keep conquering the civilized core, in a pattern that is "almost like an iron law of history" [GT 5 · 03:26](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=206) [GT 5 · 03:50](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=230).
- **Three measures.** He turns the theory into three measures: **energy, openness and cohesion**. For him they explain why the poor, mountainous state of Qin (captioned "Ting"/"Qing") united China, why Macedon beat the Greek city-states and Persia, and why Rome rose [GT 5 · 01:49](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=109) [GT 5 · 04:10](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=250) [GT 5 · 09:16](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=556).
- **A forecasting rule.** "just figure out which areas of the world are the most energetic, most open, and most cohesive". Set them against the most corrupt and divided empires, "and then you'll figure out who will conquer who" [GT 5 · 33:42](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=2022).
- **Applied to the Iran war.** The same logic drives his law of asymmetry. In March 2026 he said the US–Iran war turned on one question: "Will Iran become an energetic, open, and cohesive society?" [GT 10 · 11:40](https://youtu.be/t5oisJiorsU?t=700).

**In his big picture:** Ibn Khaldun supplies his underdog engine of history. Decline starts inside rich empires, and the hungry margins inherit the earth, because "Poverty leads to creativity" [GT 5 · 37:35](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=2255).

**In his words:** "the people in the margins, they're poorer, but because they're poorer, they're more unified" [GT 5 · 03:26](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=206)

**Reality check:** He names Ibn Khaldun only once, in GT 5. The energy–openness–cohesion scheme, and its use for modern nation-states, are Jiang's own adaptation rather than Ibn Khaldun's theory.

## Glossary

- **AI apocalypse**: His May 2026 thesis is that AI cannot become the god its builders want. It depends on human labour ("AI is based entirely on human slavery"), and its data centres are costly and easy to sabotage [GT 24 · 1:00:30](https://youtu.be/8nsxuB3Vsts?t=3630). The "real apocalypse", he says, is leaders "so convinced that AI will save the world that they will destroy it in order to make it possible" [GT 24 · 1:01:24](https://youtu.be/8nsxuB3Vsts?t=3684). In 2024 he had called AI "a scam", nothing more than supervised machine learning [GS END · 20:46](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=1246).
- **Alchemy (power as alchemy)**: Power is "the capacity to turn nothing into everything", like the alchemists' lead into gold. Banks create money from nothing, and schools make you believe in the individual and the nation-state [SH 1 · 1:09:24](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=4164). He traces all three of these concepts back to monotheism [SH 1 · 1:06:33](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=3993). Later he adds that "AI is also alchemy" [GT 28 · 58:37](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=3517).
- **Anti-civilization**: His label for Rome: "the great anti-ivilization. Or you can call it just the evil empire" [SH 21 · 00:01](https://youtu.be/Y-gXVzwbFMU?t=1). Rome's whole society was "based on war" [SH 21 · 01:42](https://youtu.be/Y-gXVzwbFMU?t=102). He contrasts it with Greece, "a civilization based on reflection on debate on openness" [SH 21 · 1:08:08](https://youtu.be/Y-gXVzwbFMU?t=4088).
- **Boomer hell**: The title of his June 2026 Substack article and livestream. Baby boomers, he says, control American politics, back Israel and the Iran war, and want to keep "the delusions of empire" [Substack Live 1 · 1:02:49](https://youtu.be/f03GAKlvZRs?t=3769) [Substack Live 1 · 1:03:17](https://youtu.be/f03GAKlvZRs?t=3797). In 2025 he said boomers want their pensions and free health care paid [GSU 8 · 08:30](https://youtu.be/E83dpuyvpiM?t=510). He gave three reasons "baby boomers suck", starting with "Baby boomers are selfish" [GSU 8 · 20:05](https://youtu.be/E83dpuyvpiM?t=1205) [GSU 8 · 20:32](https://youtu.be/E83dpuyvpiM?t=1232).
- **Bureaucracy ("death by bureaucracy")**: When an institution becomes an established brand, its managers turn to rent-seeking. Universities, for example, now exist "to promote the interests of the administrators" [SH 8 · 11:34](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=694) [SH 8 · 16:55](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=1015). Bureaucrats "make problems for everyone in order to create solutions for everyone" and "have no imagination" [SH 8 · 15:51](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=951) [SH 8 · 50:22](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=3022). - **Christian Zionism**: The belief that Christians have a duty to help Jews take control of Israel, so that Jesus can return [GS 2 · 24:59](https://youtu.be/lkKrZq4YdqY?t=1499). He calls it "the most powerful story out there", which makes its believers "the most energetic" force in US Middle East policy [GSU 5 · 23:02](https://youtu.be/5I2VPYPJJ68?t=1382) [GSU 5 · 36:03](https://youtu.be/5I2VPYPJJ68?t=2163). Doing good makes it grow and draws us back to the source [SH 9 · 44:11](https://youtu.be/9MURGjjG4aA?t=2651) [GB 1 · 02:00](https://youtu.be/TsD-8FGA84A?t=120). His examples include Homer, Dante, Jesus, Caesar and Putin [GS END · 48:29](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=2909). In 2025 he used Hegel's "world historical figure" and Nietzsche's Übermensch for the new player who appears once a game looks predictable. ## How to talk like him

1. **Open with a mystery, then answer in threes.** He frames a course as one driving question, such as "why did the US attack Iran?", then gives "the three major reasons" [GT 28 · 00:24](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=24) [GT 28 · 00:57](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=57). Wars get "three major questions" [GT 11 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/fz-Dan7NRss?t=0).
2. **Turn it into a game.** Name the players, the rules and the incentives [GT 1 · 05:43](https://youtu.be/hE4l9WyLF3U?t=343). Drop the moral language: "there's really no good and evil" [GS END · 26:04](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=1564).
3. **Ask who benefits.** "Whenever you have a problem, you always ask yourself who benefits" [SH 3 · 30:29](https://youtu.be/0g3yo1DjiLM?t=1829). That single question takes him from housing prices and immigration to "rich pensioners".
4. **Explain the big with the small.** He reaches for everyday comparisons:
   - Power is zero-sum because a classroom has only one teacher [GS END · 07:13](https://youtu.be/s_k6esWheqA?t=433).
   - Escalation is two people bumping into each other and trading insults [GT 11 · 07:12](https://youtu.be/fz-Dan7NRss?t=432).
   - The law of proximity is the game you already play for your parents' attention [GT 14 · 15:26](https://youtu.be/nOQqGy4boBY?t=926).
5. **Run a thought experiment.** "Let's imagine that we have the power to present every single story in human history" to everyone. Then ask what they remember ten years later [GSU 5 · 19:41](https://youtu.be/5I2VPYPJJ68?t=1181). Or: "Let us do a thought experiment. I'm a bank" [SH 1 · 07:38](https://youtu.be/lt8XLz78ZvY?t=458).
6. **Find the historical analogue, then check the motives.** His two-step method: first ask "are there historical analogues", then use game theory to ask why rational players would do something so stupid [GS 8 · 33:10](https://youtu.be/7y_hbz6loEo?t=1990).
