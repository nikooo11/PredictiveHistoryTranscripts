# Chapter 2: The Hebrew Bible and the Birth of Monotheism

*Draws mainly on Civilization #21–23 (December 2024), Secret History #17 and #19 (November 2025) and Great Books #3 (January 2026). Satan, the Messiah and Gog and Magog are traced through Secret History, Game Theory, the Dante sessions and the 2026 meet-ups.*

## The story he tells

Jiang calls the Bible "the most important book ever", one that is still "driving a lot of history today": you can't understand Israel, Iran or Palestine without it [Civ 21 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=0). He warns his students that his account goes against both believers and mainstream scholars, and he asks them to doubt him [Civ 21 · 00:52](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=52).

His story has three acts. **First**, the Bronze Age collapse leaves the Levant a melting pot of nomads, hill farmers, foreign mercenaries and exiled Egyptian priests. To fight the Philistines they elect Saul. Saul's mercenary David seizes the throne and, to solve the three problems every usurper faces (legitimacy, unity and differentiation), sponsors the first Bible as an "apology" [SH 17 · 04:20](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=260), [SH 17 · 05:18](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=318). A literary genius he calls the Yahwist turns this propaganda into great literature: a lonely, fallible poet-God, Yahweh, searches for a friend and finds him in the poet-king David [Civ 22 · 13:09](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=789).

**Second**, the kingdom splits and falls to Assyria and Babylon. Cut off from its land, the exiled elite's religion hardens and turns "fanatical", and prophets explain the disaster as punishment for broken covenants [Civ 21 · 33:43](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2023), [Civ 23 · 13:15](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=795).

**Third**, Cyrus the Great sends a zealous minority home as a divide-and-rule tool. Ezra and Nehemiah build a new identity around purity, the Sabbath and a single compiled Bible, and Persian Zoroastrianism adds a final battle of good against evil and a coming Messiah. That mix, he says, becomes the basis of Christianity [Civ 23 · 17:42](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=1062), [SH 19 · 31:19](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=1879). Hence his refrain: "The Jews of Persia are not the same as the Israelites of David" [SH 19 · 49:40](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=2980).

The material matters to him for three reasons. It's his model of how writing creates reality ("Poets are prophets and their words create our reality" [GB 3 · 42:56](https://youtu.be/XRP407WsA0w?t=2576)). It's his model of how empires manufacture identities. And it's a "script" that he says today's Middle East is acting out: rebuild the Temple, fight the war of Gog and Magog, await the Messiah [SH 19 · 43:09](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=2589), [GT 28 · 12:33](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=753). He ranks the Jews, Greeks and Persians together as the pillars of Western civilization [SH 17 · 1:05:37](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=3937). Running through all of it is Satan, his lens on evil, which begins as the Bible's "adversary" and ends as money and power.

## The main figures

### Yahweh

*Who:* The personal name of the God of Israel in the Hebrew Bible (written YHWH). Jews, Christians and Muslims later came to understand this God as the one God.

**What he teaches**
- Early Israel was not monotheistic. Yahweh stood at the top of a pantheon that included Baal and Asherah, and monotheism was "retroactively put back into Jewish history" [Civ 21 · 08:30](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=510), [Civ 21 · 25:16](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1516). He gives the stages: polytheism under the kings, "henotheism" (our god is the best) after the exile, and full monotheism only after Rome destroyed the Second Temple in 70 CE [Civ 23 · 04:34](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=274), [Civ 23 · 06:52](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=412).
- David made Yahweh the nation's patron god and gave him a "house", the Temple in Jerusalem. That centralized worship and gave jobs to David's allies, the Egyptian priests [SH 17 · 05:18](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=318), [SH 17 · 05:59](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=359).
- The Yahwist makes Yahweh something new, a "poet God". He creates by speaking, edits his work ("and this is good") and makes humans because he is lonely [Civ 22 · 13:09](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=789), [Civ 22 · 14:08](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=848).
- This God is fallible and learns. Jiang reads the garments God makes for Adam and Eve, the mark of Cain and the promise to Noah as God admitting his mistakes. The gods of the Iliad take vengeance, but Yahweh can reflect and forgive [SH 17 · 20:37](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1237), [SH 17 · 21:11](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1271).
- He also reports the opposite reading. In Gnostic myth, which he presents as the secret societies' view, the world was made by a monstrous demiurge, and "in the Bible the monster's name is Yahweh" [Civ 24 · 45:31](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2731). SH 5 asks: "What kind of god would kick someone out of paradise for eating a fruit?" [SH 5 · 39:49](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2389).

**In his big picture:** Yahweh is his main example of gods mirroring the rulers who promote them: a poet-God legitimizes a poet-king. The Yahweh–David friendship is also his first case of a history that "ends" with a chosen ruler, a pattern he later finds in Augustus [Civ 27 · 01:54](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=114).

**In his words:** "Yahweh is the poet God so this is a radical conception of divinity in the ancient world" [Civ 22 · 13:09](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=789)

**Reality check:** Many scholars agree that early Israelite religion included other gods and that strict monotheism took shape around the exile and the Persian period, so this part is close to the mainstream. The fallible "poet God" is his own literary reading.

### The Yahwist

*Who:* "J", the hypothetical author of the oldest strand of the Bible's first books under the Documentary Hypothesis, named for calling God "Yahweh" (German *Jahwe*). The critic Harold Bloom's *The Book of J* (1990), which Jiang cites, popularized the idea that J was a woman.

**What he teaches**
- The Bible is a spliced "library" of at least four schools: J (the House of David), E (the northern kingdom), P (the priests) and D (the Deuteronomist, who blamed Israel's fall on disloyalty to God) [Civ 21 · 40:17](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2417), [Civ 21 · 41:11](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2471). These were interleaved line by line, "a b c d", which is why he calls the Bible unreadable [Civ 21 · 42:06](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2526).
- He sets David's court historian, whose stories are "just propaganda and spin", apart from the Yahwist, a genius on the level of Homer, Plato and Dante [Civ 22 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1723). In the 2025 retelling he blurs the two and calls J "a court historian" who cleaned up David's image [SH 17 · 44:04](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2644).
- J was a woman, he argues, because she writes about childbirth, marriage and love rather than war, and because she makes woman out of "higher quality material" (flesh, where man is dust) [Civ 22 · 29:43](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1783), [SH 17 · 15:51](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=951).
- Going further than Bloom, he says she must have been David's daughter or granddaughter, since nobody of lower rank could have slipped stories that mock Yahweh past the priests. His precedent is Enheduanna, daughter of Sargon of Akkad (see Chapter 1) [Civ 22 · 30:46](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1846), [Civ 22 · 32:29](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1949).
- Her hallmarks are "economy" (a universe of meaning in a few words) and "irony" (she's funny, even about God) [Civ 22 · 50:00](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3000), [Civ 22 · 50:59](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3059). Her gaps make readers partners in "co-creation", which he says is why the Bible endures [Civ 22 · 56:02](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3362).
- He credits this story tradition for why "the most creative people in the world are Jews" (Marx, Freud, Einstein) [SH 17 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=0), [SH 17 · 47:39](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2859). He adds that "the Jews dominate" media, Hollywood and academia [SH 17 · 41:33](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2493), and that "most of your professors are Jewish" [Civ 21 · 1:05:04](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=3904).

**In his big picture:** The Yahwist shows that a poet outlasts the king who paid her. David's propaganda survives only because she made it art, which fits his claim that poets are prophets [GB 3 · 17:33](https://youtu.be/XRP407WsA0w?t=1053).

**In his words:** "the person who wrote the Bible especially Genesis was a unique literary genius of the Statue [stature] of Homer and Plato and Dante" [Civ 22 · 28:43](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1723)

**Reality check:** The four-source theory is real but much revised: many scholars now doubt a single early J and date much of the Pentateuch to the exile or later. Nothing is known of J's sex or identity, and "David's daughter" is Jiang's speculation. Jews are over-represented in some fields, but claims that they "dominate" academia and Hollywood echo old antisemitic stereotypes.

### Adam and Eve

*Who:* In Genesis 2–3, the first man and woman, placed in the Garden of Eden and expelled after eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**What he teaches**
- He begins, "you think you know the story of Adam and Eve but I guarantee you it's wrong". The familiar version, in which disobedience leads to original sin, is "completely and utterly wrong" [Civ 22 · 33:26](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2006), [Civ 22 · 35:12](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2112).
- Knowing good and evil means being able to learn from experience: you hit a table, it hurts, you stop. Add the tree of life (immortality) and you become God. So God expels the couple to guard that second tree, not to punish them [Civ 22 · 38:57](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2337), [SH 17 · 22:35](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1355).
- God lied, because the fruit wasn't poison. His analogy: a father tells his little daughters that his whiskey is poison, an uncle says it isn't, the girls drink it, and the father shouts at them and then takes them to McDonald's. The garments of skin are God's apology, and the whole thing is a "domestic comedy" [Civ 22 · 42:47](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2567), [Civ 22 · 44:30](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2670).
- Eve is the hero. The serpent speaks to her, and she judges for herself. When Adam names her "mother of all living", he is in effect saying "you are the true goddess" [SH 17 · 17:51](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1071), [SH 17 · 21:30](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1290).
- Leaving Eden can be good. Death makes creativity possible, and "Creativity is what's truly divine" [SH 17 · 23:28](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1408), [SH 17 · 24:07](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1447). He also suggests Eden is a folk memory of the egalitarian Ice Age [Civ 22 · 57:05](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3425).
- By 2026 he uses the story for geopolitics. Americans see Eve's disobedience as the root of progress; Russians see pride and a refusal to accept human limits, which is why "Russia and America cannot reconcile" [GT 23 · 44:45](https://youtu.be/6aNh6sBpqvQ?t=2685), [GT 23 · 45:24](https://youtu.be/6aNh6sBpqvQ?t=2724).

**In his big picture:** Eden opens the "circle" of sacred history: humanity leaves the garden, and Christianity promises the return [Civ 23 · 46:49](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=2809). It also backs his attack on schooling that forbids mistakes: "school is stupid" [SH 17 · 09:38](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=578). For Milton's retelling, see Chapter 9.

**In his words:** "the real hero of the story is actually the woman" [SH 17 · 17:51](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1071)

**Reality check:** Original sin as a doctrine comes from Paul and Augustine rather than Genesis alone (see Chapter 5), and Jewish readings never stressed it, so part of his point is widely shared. The Ice Age link is speculation.

### Cain and Abel

*Who:* In Genesis 4, the sons of Adam and Eve. Cain, a farmer, kills his brother Abel, a shepherd, after God favors Abel's offering.

**What he teaches**
- He tells it as a family comedy. God loves Abel's mutton and scorns Cain's vegetables, like a parent praising one child's gift and telling the other "you suck". "God is being a terrible parent" [Civ 22 · 46:53](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2813), [Civ 22 · 47:54](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2874).
- After the murder God doesn't take an eye for an eye [SH 17 · 26:14](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1574). Cain protests that exile will get him killed, and God gives him a protective mark. Jiang voices God here as conceding "this is partly my fault" [SH 17 · 27:07](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1627).
- So Yahweh is "a god of forgiveness, of reconciliation, of compromise, of debate", unlike the Iliad, whose central problem is the lack of forgiveness [SH 17 · 26:36](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1596), [SH 17 · 27:23](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1643).
- The lesson is that faith means arguing with God, because the arguer helps a fallible God grow [Civ 22 · 48:51](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2931).

**In his big picture:** Cain is his main evidence that the Hebrew God can be argued with, which he treats as the root of a Jewish culture of debate.

**In his words:** "Faith love of God requires you to argue with God because it's only through the process of argument that God who's fallible will learn and grow" [Civ 22 · 48:51](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=2931)

### Noah

*Who:* In Genesis 6–9, the righteous man God tells to build an ark before a flood destroys the world. Afterward God promises never to do it again.

**What he teaches**
- No trace of Noah's Ark or a world flood has been found. The long search for them is part of his proof that the Bible is literature, not a historical record [Civ 21 · 09:36](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=576), [Civ 22 · 08:16](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=496).
- The covenant with Noah is God's remorse: as long as humans aren't too evil, he won't destroy the world again [Civ 22 · 15:08](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=908). In 2025 he voices God as saying "Oops, I made a mistake again", accepting that free will brings some evil [SH 17 · 28:42](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1722).
- It's the first of four covenants (Noah, Abraham, Moses, David). The first three are conditional; David's is not [Civ 22 · 16:00](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=960).
- He points out the puzzle: God drowned the world for its wickedness, yet people stayed wicked. The "secret societies'" answer, from the Book of Enoch, is that the flood was aimed at the Nephilim [SH 5 · 32:51](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=1971), [SH 5 · 34:14](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2054).

**In his big picture:** Noah shows God learning. In Secret History a flood becomes a cosmic "failsafe" that resets the world when evil goes too far [SH 9 · 1:05:26](https://youtu.be/9MURGjjG4aA?t=3926).

**In his words:** "after Yahweh destroys the world he feels tremendous remorse for what he's done" [Civ 22 · 15:08](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=908)

### Abraham

*Who:* The patriarch of Genesis 12–25, from Ur in Mesopotamia, to whom God promises land and countless descendants. Jews, Christians and Muslims all honor him as their father in faith.

**What he teaches**
- The Bible dates Abraham to about 2000 BCE, but there's no evidence of anything before David, so Jiang counts David, not Abraham, as Israel's founder [Civ 21 · 31:30](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1890), [Civ 21 · 32:43](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1963).
- Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were probably three tribal ancestors merged into one family tree when their tribes allied. Jacob's tribe was strongest (he names the nation), and Isaac gets the least attention [Civ 22 · 20:01](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1201), [Civ 22 · 20:56](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1256).
- The covenant exchanges loyalty to Yahweh for a nation stretching from the Nile to the Euphrates, sealed by circumcision [Civ 22 · 02:16](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=136). Circumcision was an Egyptian priestly skill, so it gave the Egyptian priests power in Israel [Civ 22 · 23:50](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1430).
- Abraham bargains with God over Sodom (50 righteous people, then 45, down to 10). A mortal tells God he's wrong, and God agrees. For Jiang this is friendship, not slavery, and it is why "the basis of Judaism is debate and argument and questions" [SH 17 · 31:24](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1884), [SH 17 · 33:14](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1994).
- Through Hagar and Ishmael the Arabs are heirs of Abraham too, which let Muhammad claim the promised land for them [Civ 28 · 04:57](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=297).
- Today, he says, a "fanatical faction" in Israel's government wants a "Greater Israel" from the Nile to the Euphrates on the strength of this promise. That would require pushing America out of the Middle East [GSU 2 · 09:20](https://youtu.be/N4cs-8mrP_s?t=560), [GSU 2 · 10:21](https://youtu.be/N4cs-8mrP_s?t=621).

**In his big picture:** Abraham supplies the covenant logic (loyalty in exchange for land) that Jiang reads as a live political script (see Chapter 12).

**In his words:** "it's not like master servant. It's more like friend because what a friend does is a friend is honest with you." [SH 17 · 32:46](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=1966)

**Reality check:** Historians broadly agree there's no archaeological evidence for the patriarchs. Expansive "Greater Israel" ideas circulate on Israel's religious right, but borders from the Nile to the Euphrates are not official Israeli policy.

### Jacob

*Who:* Isaac's younger son (Genesis 25–50). He takes his twin Esau's birthright, flees to his uncle Laban, marries Laban's daughters Leah and Rachel, and is renamed Israel. His twelve sons found the twelve tribes.

**What he teaches**
- Jacob is the Bible's real founding father. He gives Israel its name, and his tribe was the strongest in the merged family tree [Civ 22 · 03:18](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=198), [Civ 22 · 20:56](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1256).
- He calls Jacob and Rachel the Bible's greatest love story, perhaps history's [SH 17 · 34:34](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2074).
- He reads between the lines. Leah must have hated Rachel and nagged their father Laban for seven years, so swapping the brides was a harassed father's way out [Civ 22 · 54:06](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3246), [SH 17 · 37:01](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2221).
- Both lovers prove their love by enduring: Jacob works fourteen years, and Rachel waits rather than marry someone else [Civ 22 · 55:08](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3308), [SH 17 · 38:14](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2294).
- The sisters' baby race, fought through their maids, is "all just a comedy", and "Only a woman would write stories like this" [SH 17 · 41:27](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2487), [SH 17 · 38:55](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2335).
- In the Dante sessions he retells it as "pure poetry", with Rachel watching Jacob "with longing eyes" (see Chapter 7 for Dante's dream of Leah and Rachel) [Dante 12 · 1:20:04](https://youtu.be/Sa5kffYLvBE?t=4804), [Dante 12 · 1:20:46](https://youtu.be/Sa5kffYLvBE?t=4846).

**In his big picture:** Jacob's story is his showcase for the Yahwist's "economy": a few verses from which a reader can imagine fourteen years of family drama.

**In his words:** "the greatest love story in the Bible. You can also argue it's the greatest love story ever in human history" [SH 17 · 34:34](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2074)

### Moses

*Who:* The prophet of Exodus–Deuteronomy who leads the Israelites out of Egyptian slavery, receives the Law (including the Ten Commandments) at Sinai, and dies in sight of the promised land.

**What he teaches**
- No Egyptian record mentions Moses, the plagues or the parting of the sea [Civ 22 · 09:08](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=548). The stories don't even connect: "David has no memory of Moses" [Civ 22 · 10:04](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=604).
- The Moses story is a founding myth for the Egyptian priests in David's coalition, explaining why they're in Israel and control the Temple sacrifices. "Moses" is an Egyptian name meaning "son of" (as in Ramesses), and so is Aaron's [Civ 22 · 22:00](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1320), [Civ 22 · 22:56](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1376).
- Under Ezra the Bible's hero switched from David the king to Moses the priestly lawgiver, because the Persian-era community was ruled by priests [Civ 23 · 55:38](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=3338). The "law of Moses", written and oral, became the core of later Judaism [SH 26 · 03:00](https://youtu.be/kULUM_eB8KI?t=180).
- Moses is the most-mentioned figure in the Quran, and Muhammad followed his pattern of leading a people back to a promised land (see Chapter 5) [Civ 28 · 03:51](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=231).
- Moses seeing the promised land without entering it is Jiang's image for Dante's despair before the final cantos (see Chapter 7) [Dante 4 · 08:11](https://youtu.be/brRNCLD7to0?t=491).

**In his big picture:** Moses shows the Bible as "political real estate". Every faction needed an ancestor in it, and when power passed from kings to priests, the lawgiver displaced the king.

**In his words:** "what Moses is doing is explaining why there are Egyptian priests in the nation of Israel" [Civ 22 · 22:00](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1320)

**Reality check:** Most historians agree there is no direct evidence for the Exodus as told, and an Egyptian origin for the name Moses is widely accepted. The idea that the story encodes exiled Egyptian priests is speculative, though it echoes older theories such as Freud's *Moses and Monotheism*.

### Saul

*Who:* The first king of Israel (1 Samuel), anointed by the prophet Samuel. He falls out with David and dies fighting the Philistines on Mount Gilboa.

**What he teaches**
- A diverse coalition elected Saul to fight the Philistines, and he was a good king and military leader [Civ 21 · 19:50](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1190).
- David was one of his mercenaries. He fell out with Saul, joined the Philistines, and came back after Saul and his sons died in battle, fighting a civil war against the House of Saul [Civ 21 · 21:00](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1260), [Civ 21 · 21:52](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1312).
- In the cave story David cuts a piece from Saul's robe but spares him. Jiang calls it spin to prove David had no ambition: "obviously this story cannot be true" [Civ 21 · 50:25](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=3025), [Civ 21 · 51:16](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=3076).
- In 2025 he goes further and says the popular mercenary killed Saul to become king [SH 17 · 43:20](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=2600).

**In his big picture:** Saul is the legitimate, elected king whose memory creates David's problem, and so the reason the Bible was written.

**In his words:** "We elected so [Saul] as king but you stole the throne from him" [SH 17 · 04:34](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=274)

**Reality check:** In 1 Samuel, Saul falls on his own sword at Gilboa while David is elsewhere. That David killed him is Jiang's inference; no source says so.

### David

*Who:* King of Israel (traditionally c. 1000 BCE). He is the shepherd who kills Goliath, makes Jerusalem his capital and receives God's promise of an eternal dynasty, and he is traditionally the author of many Psalms.

**What he teaches**
- David, not Abraham, founded Israel, and he is the first biblical figure with archaeological support [Civ 21 · 32:43](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=1963). He was a charismatic foreign mercenary who took the throne, a pattern Jiang also finds in Sargon and Genghis Khan (see Chapter 1) [SH 13 · 45:42](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=2742).
- Every usurper needs an "apology", a sponsored account saying God forced his hand. The first Bible was David's, and writing then was as costly as "movie making today" [Civ 21 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2247), [Civ 21 · 38:26](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2306).
- The Abner story is spin. The Bible says David feasted Abner and Joab killed him in a private feud. ### Cyrus the Great

*Who:* Founder of the Persian (Achaemenid) Empire, reigning c. 559–530 BCE, who took Babylon in 539 BCE. The Bible credits him with letting the exiled Judeans go home and rebuild their Temple, and Isaiah calls him God's "anointed".

**What he teaches**

## Key ideas from these lectures

- **Apology**: a text a usurper sponsors to explain that God, not ambition, made him king. The first Bible was David's [Civ 21 · 37:27](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2247).
- **Legitimacy, unity, differentiation**: the three jobs writing does for a new ruler (why I rule, why we're one family, why we're not them). He applies it to David, Augustus and Charlemagne [SH 17 · 1:04:00](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=3840), [Civ 34 · 47:11](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=2831).
- **Political real estate**: everyone wants a place in the Bible because it confers legitimacy, so rival texts were merged rather than cut [Civ 21 · 44:34](https://youtu.be/RkpGrRUgjnk?t=2674).
- **Cosmology, not chronology**: the Bible's "history" is a family tree built to unite a coalition. "Israel it's a literary creation" [Civ 22 · 10:58](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=658), [Civ 22 · 26:45](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1605).
- **Syncretization**: merging peoples by marrying their gods and turning their ancestors into fathers and sons [Civ 22 · 19:02](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1142).
- **Poet-king / poet-God**: David's legitimacy rests on being a sensitive poet, not a warrior or priest, matching a God who creates with words [Civ 22 · 12:10](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=730).
- **Economy and irony**: the Yahwist's two techniques, meaning packed into few words, plus humor [Civ 22 · 50:00](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=3000).
- **Spin / gaslighting**: retelling a crime so the audience forgets the real one [SH 17 · 1:01:26](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=3686), [SH 17 · 54:15](https://youtu.be/PlMNq44tBG0?t=3255).
- **Diaspora hardening**: a religion cut off from its land grows rigid, like overseas Chinese communities that keep traditional characters [Civ 23 · 12:12](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=732).
- **Divide and rule**: Persia's method, which he says America uses in the Middle East today [SH 19 · 1:08:13](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=4093).
- **Israelites, Jews, Israelis**: three different groups. The Israelites of David's time are "gone", Jews practice Judaism, and Israelis are citizens of Israel [SH 19 · 1:10:15](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=4215).
- **Grandness, completeness, unity**: his test for ranking religions like stories. Completeness needs an ending (eschatology), which Zoroastrianism supplied [Civ 23 · 35:55](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=2155), [Civ 23 · 40:48](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=2448).
- **Messiah**: "the anointed one". First Cyrus, then the son of David who will lead Israel in a final battle; Christians say Jesus. Failed messiahs are explained away as false messiahs or antichrists (see Glossary) [Civ 23 · 57:37](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=3457), [GT 28 · 20:56](https://youtu.be/dja6dkCfngE?t=1256).
- **The Bible as script**: read a people's sacred text to predict what they will do [SH 19 · 43:31](https://youtu.be/3t0MeVBD24I?t=2611).
