# Chapter 8: Vikings, Mongols, the Reformation, Science and Conquest

*Drawn mainly from Civilization #31, #35–39, #41–45, #49 and #50 (February–May 2025) and Geo-Strategy Update #4, "Newton's Divine Plan" (June 2025), with later echoes from the Secret History, Game Theory and Dante series.*

## The story he tells

These lectures cover the second semester of his Beijing high-school course, and they all hang on a model he unveiled on its first day: the "oceanic currents of history" [Civ 31 · 27:13](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=1633), [Civ 35 · 31:47](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=1907). Empires expand, and as they trade with the isolated borderlands at their edges, hire them as mercenaries and get raided by them, they pour energy into those places. The borderlands grow richer and more crowded, run short of status positions, and burst outward like hurricanes. Sometimes they swallow the empire [Civ 31 · 35:35](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=2135), [Civ 31 · 38:44](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=2324). Borderland cultures prize "freedom egalitarianism and self-reliance", which is how Vikings, Mongols and Arabs beat far larger states [Civ 31 · 57:18](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=3438). Empires have mass, organization and strategic depth. Borderlands have energy, openness and opportunism [Civ 35 · 33:51](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2031), [Civ 35 · 36:51](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2211).

The second half of the course asks why poor, divided Europe ended up ruling the world. His answer is culture, not technology. China invented paper, printing, the compass and gunpowder, but its bureaucracy "did nothing whatsoever" with them [Civ 38 · 1:01:19](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=3679). Europe, always at war with itself, had to "innovate or you will get destroyed by your neighbor" [Civ 45 · 56:35](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=3395). Europe copied the Islamic Golden Age through three movements: the Renaissance, the Reformation and the Scientific Revolution [Civ 37 · 1:09:31](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=4171). He credits all three ultimately to Dante (see Chapters 6–7) [Civ 43 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=0).

The figures below are his case studies. Luther, Calvin and Weber show how religious anxiety became capitalism. Galileo and Kuhn show science rising by asking "how do we know truth" [Civ 43 · 12:39](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=759), then hardening into a bureaucracy. Genghis Khan, Cortés and Pizarro show that conquest is psychological warfare. Vermeer and William of Orange show the Dutch and British building the middle class, the corporation and the central bank. Newton closes the chapter as a bridge to the present. In Jiang's telling, Newton's biblical prophecy became the Christian Zionist plan he says is driving war in the Middle East [GSU 4 · 09:53](https://youtu.be/Kw-TiN6dEcM?t=593).

## The main figures

### The Vikings

*Who:* Seafaring Norse people of Denmark, Norway and Sweden who raided, traded and settled across Europe in the Viking Age (793–1066), from Newfoundland to the Volga.

**What he teaches**
- They deserve to be a fifth pillar of Western civilization, beside the Greeks, Romans, Jews and Christians, because they founded or shaped Britain, France, Germany and Russia, the dominant cultures of the last 500 years [Civ 35 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=0), [Civ 35 · 10:48](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=648).
- Most Vikings were traders and mercenaries, not raiders. They were poor and egalitarian: on a longship you cannot tell who the captain is. They also ran a slave trade, selling fellow pagans to Christians and Muslims, who could not enslave their own believers [Civ 35 · 12:50](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=770), [Civ 35 · 22:25](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=1345), [Civ 35 · 27:10](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=1630).
- The raids began from opportunism. Monasteries were Europe's universities and banks, stuffed with gold and left unguarded because God supposedly protected them. Rivers were open because armoured knights "do not like water" [Civ 35 · 46:11](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2771), [Civ 35 · 48:03](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2883). Feudal Europe refused to adapt its tactics because that would have meant changing its hierarchy [Civ 35 · 50:07](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=3007).
- The Viking Age ended when rich Vikings were co-opted: they married into the European nobility and converted to Christianity [Civ 35 · 50:59](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=3059).
- For Vikings the community is "a set of stories", and a person matters by adding a memorable story through adventure. He illustrates this with two Georgetown students who drove 24 hours to urinate in Canada [Civ 36 · 09:02](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=542), [Civ 36 · 11:08](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=668).
- He claims pagan Vikings had no concept of homosexuality ("no one cared") and that women gained status through magic [Civ 35 · 1:07:05](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=4025).
- He maps Viking courage, loyalty and resourcefulness onto Protestantism [Civ 42 · 39:42](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=2382). He would rather be a Viking's slave than a Mongol's, because Vikings were curious about other cultures [Civ 39 · 1:01:56](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3716).

**In his big picture:** The Vikings are his model borderland people, and their values run through his account of the Reformation, the British Empire and Russia.

**In his words:** "if I had a choice whether to send my child to Yale or to Viking school I would send my kid to Viking school" [Civ 35 · 43:34](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2614)

**Reality check:** Old Norse law and sagas treated taking the passive role in sex between men as a grave insult (*níð*), which cuts against "no one cared". Whether Scandinavian Rus founded the first Russian state is also still debated.

### Odin, Thor and Loki

*Who:* Gods of Norse mythology, known mainly from the Eddas that Christian Icelanders wrote down in the 13th century. Odin is the one-eyed all-father of wisdom and war, Thor the thunder god with the hammer Mjölnir, and Loki the shape-shifting trickster.

**What he teaches**
- He calls Norse mythology the greatest in history. It is grand (nine realms joined by the world tree, with the Norns steering fate), complete (the cosmos is built from the giant Ymir's body and ends at Ragnarök) and unified [Civ 36 · 12:53](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=773), [Civ 36 · 13:51](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=831). Christian editors added survivors after Ragnarök. The pagan version, where everything dies, teaches you to cherish every day [Civ 36 · 15:47](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=947).
- Each story teaches one of three Viking values [Civ 36 · 18:00](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=1080). **Courage:** Odin gives an eye to drink from the well of cosmic knowledge, then kills himself to see the realm of death and resurrects [Civ 36 · 42:06](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2526). **Loyalty:** the war god Tyr puts his hand in the wolf Fenrir's mouth, knowing he will lose it [Civ 36 · 44:32](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2672). **Resourcefulness:** Loki turns himself into a mare to lure away the giant builder's horse and save the gods from a bad bargain [Civ 36 · 45:16](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2716), [Civ 36 · 46:57](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2817).
- He enjoys the comedy too. When a frost giant steals Thor's hammer, Loki dresses Thor as the bride Freya, and Thor belches through the wedding feast before smashing the giant king [Civ 36 · 47:51](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2871), [Civ 36 · 48:39](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2919).
- The myths live on in Wagner's *Ring* cycle and Tolkien [Civ 36 · 16:50](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=1010). He later retells Wagner's plot with Wotan (captioned "Waton" and "warton"), Odin's German name (see Chapter 9) [Civ 54 · 35:51](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2151).
- Their power came from oral tradition. Stories told by the fire in huge dark halls were "clay" that every teller reshaped. We lost that through literacy, Christianity's focus on good and evil, and hierarchy [Civ 36 · 53:03](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=3183), [Civ 36 · 1:01:34](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=3694).

**In his big picture:** The gods show his claim that mythology is a culture's collective subconscious, and that oral cultures are more imaginative than literate ones.

**In his words:** "Odin personifies the idea of courage in the Viking world to seek the unknown relentlessly" [Civ 36 · 42:53](https://youtu.be/tYMNsa83NHY?t=2573)

### Ibn Khaldun

*Who:* North African historian and statesman (1332–1406). His *Muqaddimah* explains the rise and fall of dynasties through *asabiyyah*, or group solidarity.

**What he teaches**
- He introduces Ibn Khaldun (captioned "iben Cen", "even Coden") as the father of social science, who invented much of economics, quantitative history and political science [Civ 37 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=1487).
- In his reading, *asabiyyah* means social cohesion. Borderlands conquer empires because their people are more egalitarian, free and cohesive, while big empires lose cohesion [Civ 37 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=1487).
- He uses Ibn Khaldun to show that Islamic creativity survived the Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258 [Civ 37 · 23:49](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=1429). He says plainly that Ibn Khaldun "is a major inspiration" for his own history [Civ 37 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=1487).

**In his big picture:** *Asabiyyah* is the seed of his borderland-versus-empire theory.

**In his words:** "the Borderlands are able to conquer Empires because the Borderlands the people there have asaba which just means that they are more egalitarian more free more cohesive" [Civ 37 · 24:47](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=1487)

### Zhu Yuanzhang (the Hongwu Emperor)

*Who:* Born a poor peasant (1328–1398), he rose through rebellion against the Mongol Yuan and founded the Ming dynasty in 1368, reigning as the Hongwu Emperor. The captions spell his name "Jan Jang", "jul Yan Jang" and "Julian Jang".

**What he teaches**
- He is Jiang's example of a strong dynastic founder who can still tax effectively, before the long decline that Yuhua Wang charts [Civ 38 · 24:35](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=1475).
- Under the Song, Abbasid sea trade had pulled China's largest cities to the coast. Zhu feared the coast would grow rich enough to depose him and the poorer interior would rebel, so he banned maritime trade, making China "much more insular and weak and poor" [Civ 38 · 33:19](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=1999), [Civ 38 · 34:19](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=2059). "for China the priority is internal stability rather than wealth" [Civ 38 · 35:14](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=2114).
- He restored the *keju* civil-service exam, which the Mongols had dropped. When southerners swept the results and northerners cried corruption, his chief minister reported that the exam had been fair. Zhu then killed the minister, his investigators, the examiners and the top scorers [Civ 38 · 1:04:39](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=3879), [Civ 38 · 1:07:25](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=4045).
- The moral is that the *keju* "is not a meritocracy". It was a quota system that set local elites against each other, which he calls divide and conquer [Civ 38 · 44:12](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=2652). Emperors hardly matter, he adds, because the bureaucracy rules: "you can have I don't know Julian Jang who cares" [Civ 45 · 1:03:47](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=3827).

**In his big picture:** Zhu embodies Jiang's thesis that imperial bureaucracy trades innovation for stability, which is why China's great inventions transformed Europe, not China.

**In his words:** "the Emperor does not want a fair open and transparent system he wants a system in which the localities will not rebel against the center" [Civ 38 · 1:07:25](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=4045)

**Reality check:** The story matches the real 1397 *North–South List* case, in which Hongwu executed officials and passed a new list of northern candidates. Most historians see the *keju* as partly meritocratic, with regional quotas formalized afterwards, rather than as designed purely to divide elites.

### Genghis Khan

*Who:* Genghis Khan (born Temüjin, c. 1162–1227) united the Mongol tribes in 1206 and began the conquests that built the largest contiguous land empire in history. The captions spell his name many ways, including "gangas Khan", "gas Khan" and "Genghask".

**What he teaches**
- The Mongols were the most successful wave of steppe peoples, after the Yamnaya, the Xiongnu/Huns and the Turks. Culturally they were closer to the Yamnaya than to the Chinese [Civ 39 · 06:21](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=381), [Civ 39 · 09:19](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=559).
- *The Secret History of the Mongols* is "suspicious" because it follows the Proto-Indo-European hero myth. The god-chosen hero suffers hardship, sacrifices his beloved (Genghis kills his blood brother Jamukha, whom Jiang reads as a lover) and then shatters the old world [Civ 39 · 19:21](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=1161), [Civ 39 · 25:42](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=1542). The Jesus story inverts this pattern (see Chapter 5) [Civ 39 · 28:15](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=1695).
- Genghis fits his "great conqueror" pattern alongside Sargon, Philip II and Julius Caesar (see Chapters 1, 3–4). Each won over mentors (for Genghis, Jamukha and Toghrul) and then destroyed them, relied on a brilliant deputy (Subutai), believed in a divine mission, and built a professional, meritocratic, innovative army [Civ 39 · 34:53](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2093), [Civ 39 · 36:38](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2198).
- He uses game theory to explain Mongol brutality. Few in number, fighting far from home and unable to govern, the Mongols needed three things. First, escalation dominance: they razed any city that killed their envoys. Second, terror, including against themselves: if one soldier fled, his whole unit of ten was executed. Third, an aura of invincibility: people said they were demons from Tartarus, or hell, which he says is where the name Tartar comes from [Civ 39 · 43:06](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2586), [Civ 39 · 47:28](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2848), [Civ 39 · 49:14](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2954), [Civ 39 · 51:02](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3062).
- The empire collapsed for the same reasons it rose. A free, egalitarian culture despised the hierarchies it conquered. From China's human-wave warfare the Mongols had learned that "people are an infinite resource". In Yuan China, Mongol nobles who refused to assimilate clashed with emperors who knew they had to [Civ 39 · 54:02](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3242), [Civ 39 · 56:56](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3416), [Civ 39 · 1:04:42](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3882).
- Before Genghis the Mongols were poor mercenaries for the Song, and "it's usually a great leader" who changes that [GT 5 · 54:07](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=3247), [GT 5 · 54:28](https://youtu.be/ybufqRY77PQ?t=3268). Japan survived not because of the kamikaze wind but because the Mongols found no local allies there to divide and conquer [Civ 58 · 51:20](https://youtu.be/tquo1UsC5Nw?t=3080).

**In his big picture:** The Mongols are his textbook borderland hurricane, and the classroom demonstration of game theory that he reuses for the conquistadors.

**In his words:** "according to Game Theory everything the Mongols did made complete sense okay in fact it's their optimal strategy" [Civ 39 · 51:48](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=3108)

**Reality check:** The claim that one in 200 men descends from him comes from a 2003 genetic study, and the link to Genghis himself is an inference. His claim that the Mongols left no cultural legacy is contested. Historians credit the Pax Mongolica with major transfers of trade, knowledge and technology, and Jiang himself credits it with carrying gunpowder west [Civ 45 · 14:49](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=889).

### The Medici

*Who:* Florentine banking dynasty that dominated Florence from the 1430s, later ruled it as dukes, produced four popes, and patronized many of the leading Renaissance artists.

**What he teaches**
- The gold florin became Europe's trading currency. Cosimo de' Medici pooled rich Florentines' money into a bank with branches across Europe, taking a cut of continental trade. The family produced four popes and made itself Florence's monarchy [Civ 41 · 09:00](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=540), [Civ 41 · 09:46](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=586).
- Merchant elites have a legitimacy problem: they did not win power in battle and do not speak for God. So the Medici bought legitimacy with culture, funding the cathedral and patronizing Leonardo, Raphael, Michelangelo, Botticelli and Donatello [Civ 41 · 03:49](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=229), [Civ 41 · 12:37](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=757).
- The Medici made Galileo their court scientist [Civ 43 · 30:16](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=1816). He also says they tortured Machiavelli, a staunch democrat [Civ 41 · 14:54](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=894).
- Bankers make kings. In Jiang's account, the House of York borrowed from the Medici, Richard III refused to repay, and so the Medici sponsored Henry Tudor's invasion of England [Civ 50 · 19:40](https://youtu.be/laGSxfvlOfk?t=1180).

**In his big picture:** The Medici begin a thread he follows to the Bank of England and later financiers (see Chapter 10): money must buy legitimacy, and it can unmake rulers who default.

**In his words:** "if you're a merchant family you have a legitimacy problem You didn't win the title you didn't win your wealth on the battlefield You don't represent God" [Civ 41 · 09:46](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=586)

**Reality check:** Edward IV's Yorkist regime did borrow heavily from the Medici bank. Standard accounts, however, credit the French crown with backing Henry Tudor's 1485 invasion. The Medici link is Jiang's.

### Leonardo da Vinci

*Who:* Tuscan painter, engineer and inventor (1452–1519), the archetypal "Renaissance man". He painted *The Last Supper* in Milan and the *Mona Lisa*.

**What he teaches**
- He calls Leonardo a polymath who fused science, philosophy, music and poetry into his art [Civ 41 · 11:39](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=699).
- *The Last Supper* marks the leap from medieval art, which was "blinding and awesome" and made you submit, to Renaissance art, which is "compelling and curious" and invites you in. There are no halos, the table seems to extend towards the viewer, and every disciple anxiously wonders whether he is the traitor [Civ 41 · 21:30](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1290), [Civ 41 · 22:29](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1349), [Civ 41 · 23:31](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1411).
- Leonardo read souls through hands, faces and necks. Judas sits in shadow, clutching coins, his neck too tense to speak [Civ 41 · 25:22](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1522), [Civ 41 · 26:18](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1578). The calm Jesus, who has already forgiven Judas, expresses an idea Leonardo got from Dante: God's love never gives up on anyone [Civ 41 · 29:15](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1755), [Civ 41 · 30:10](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1810). He dismisses *The Da Vinci Code* as "no scholarship" but still enjoys its Mary Magdalene reading [Civ 41 · 27:16](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1636).
- The *Mona Lisa* is "secretly alive": looked at directly she is neutral, and as you move away she smiles [Civ 41 · 31:01](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1861). In the 2026 Dante sessions it is his model of art you could look at every day for the rest of your life [Dante 6 · 1:40:18](https://youtu.be/e_9fndobOnI?t=6018). He also points out that Leonardo was a commoner [Dante 12 · 20:22](https://youtu.be/Sa5kffYLvBE?t=1222).

**In his big picture:** Leonardo is humanism made visible: art that needs the viewer's imagination, a lineage Jiang traces back to Dante.

**In his words:** "the art is alive if you engage with it Okay art is not meant to be blinding and awesome It's meant to be here and now" [Civ 41 · 31:58](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1918)

**Reality check:** He says scholars accept that the bread and hands encode music, and adds a "33133" Bible-verse reading [Civ 41 · 28:19](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1699). Both are enthusiasts' claims, not scholarly consensus, and the Dante influence is his own thesis.

### Michelangelo

*Who:* Florentine sculptor, painter and architect (1475–1564), creator of the *David* and of the Sistine Chapel ceiling, which includes the *Creation of Adam*.

**What he teaches**
- Michelangelo lived with the Medici for a time [Civ 41 · 10:39](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=639). Raphael painted the famously reclusive artist into *The School of Athens* as the gloomy philosopher Heraclitus [Civ 41 · 38:32](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=2312).
- The *Creation of Adam* can be read two ways. Either God made Adam nearly his equal, or "God is Adam's creation". The shape behind God and his angels is a human brain, so this God is "an emanation from our imagination" [Civ 41 · 1:11:50](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=4310), [Civ 41 · 1:12:43](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=4363).
- Painting this on the Sistine ceiling planted Dante's idea at the heart of the Catholic Church. This, he says, is how Dante managed to "destroy an empire peacefully" [Civ 41 · 1:13:37](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=4417), [Civ 41 · 1:14:28](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=4468). Florence later buried Galileo beside him [Civ 43 · 36:22](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=2182).

**In his big picture:** Michelangelo is his visual proof that the Renaissance made human imagination the real creative force.

**In his words:** "so the real god is the human imagination the human brain the human mind That's what gives life to the universe" [Civ 41 · 1:13:37](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=4417)

**Reality check:** The hidden-brain reading was proposed by the physician Frank Meshberger in 1990. It is a popular hypothesis, not established intent.

### Martin Luther

*Who:* German friar and theology professor (1483–1546) whose Ninety-five Theses (1517) against indulgences began the Protestant Reformation.

**What he teaches**
- Catholicism rested on orthodoxy, hierarchy and "justification by works", which bred disconnection, corruption and hypocrisy. Protestantism answered with direct access to the Bible, equality before God and justification by faith [Civ 42 · 00:53](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=53), [Civ 42 · 04:02](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=242). But faith brings anxiety, because how do you know you truly believe? [Civ 42 · 05:13](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=313).
- The Theses attacked indulgences sold to build St Peter's Basilica. In Jiang's paraphrase, Luther says the Pope insults God by implying God can be bribed, and asks why the richest man in Europe does not pay for the building himself [Civ 42 · 24:30](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1470), [Civ 42 · 26:18](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1578), [Civ 42 · 27:00](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1620).
- Wycliffe and Hus had failed, but Luther survived because German princes wanted to divorce themselves financially from Rome [Civ 42 · 23:36](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1416).
- He opened "a Pandora's box". About 300,000 peasants rose up against feudalism, in a "protocommunist" revolt that the nobility massacred, and denominations multiplied [Civ 42 · 29:34](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1774), [Civ 42 · 30:38](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1838).
- Protestants beat richer Catholic Europe with three inventions. The printing press brought literacy, the musket let committed commoners defeat knights, and banknotes let hard work be stored without limit [Civ 42 · 42:41](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=2561), [Civ 42 · 44:11](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=2651).
- Much later he sets Luther's justification by faith beside Sabbatai Zevi and Jacob Frank's idea of proving faith by sinning (see Chapter 10) [SH 26 · 1:13:30](https://youtu.be/kULUM_eB8KI?t=4410).

**In his big picture:** Luther lights the fuse. The anxiety of justification by faith, sharpened by Calvin, becomes the psychological engine of capitalism.

**In his words:** "Martin Luther says that's stupid. The church doesn't have authority to decide who goes to heaven." [SH 26 · 1:13:30](https://youtu.be/kULUM_eB8KI?t=4410)

### John Calvin

*Who:* French theologian (1509–1564) who made Geneva the centre of Reformed Protestantism. His teaching on predestination shaped the Dutch, Scottish, Huguenot and Puritan churches.

**What he teaches**
- Calvin was a Frenchman educated at the Sorbonne who fled to Geneva, where the early converts were the aspirational middle class of the independent Swiss towns [Civ 42 · 28:46](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1726).
- Double predestination was a weapon against the Church: if God settled everyone's fate at the start of time, the Church cannot sell you heaven [Civ 42 · 12:16](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=736), [GMU 3 · 27:59](https://youtu.be/5rDMq-5_xkM?t=1679).

## Key ideas from these lectures

- **Oceanic currents of history**: history as an ocean of cultural ecosystems whose currents collide like hurricanes. It is neither a cycle nor a line, carries no moral judgment, and "once you unleash them they must destroy" [Civ 31 · 27:13](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=1633), [Civ 31 · 1:01:30](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=3690).
- **Borderlands versus empire**: empires have mass, organization and strategic depth. Borderlands have energy, openness and opportunism, and they are "Shameless in taking advantage of those who you can" [Civ 35 · 36:51](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2211), [Civ 35 · 38:50](https://youtu.be/7k5XoLzwmls?t=2330). Gunpowder reversed the balance, because it rewards organization [Civ 45 · 12:02](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=722).
- **Three boundary conditions**: elite overproduction, rat utopia (the old never make room for the young) and financialization (debt slavery) doom every prosperous society. He applies them to Rome, China and the Maya [Civ 31 · 40:32](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=2432), [Civ 44 · 17:39](https://youtu.be/-DnfGcvZrfA?t=1059).
- **Open cooperative competition**: innovation comes from rivals with no central authority who still trade and intermarry, as in the Warring States, Italy and Britain. A unified bureaucracy kills it [Civ 38 · 03:09](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=189), [Civ 45 · 19:30](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=1170).
- **Culture beats technology**: "technology does not matter you can steal the technology what matters is the culture". Chinese bureaucrats kept a monopoly over status, literacy and culture, and he calls Confucianism "bureaucratism" [Civ 38 · 1:01:19](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=3679), [Civ 38 · 1:03:39](https://youtu.be/A2LMJerHcKM?t=3819).
- **Optimal strategy and the ultimate taboo**: game theory asks what each player's best move is under its constraints. Weak conquerors use escalation dominance, terror and an aura of invincibility, and of whoever breaks the ultimate taboo he says "you become God yourself" [Civ 39 · 45:31](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2731), [Civ 44 · 48:34](https://youtu.be/-DnfGcvZrfA?t=2914).
- **Whole-society approach**: "the nature of the military will determine the nature of your political system" [Civ 45 · 03:02](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=182). Gunpowder forced Europe to move from feudalism to the nation-state, from farm to town, and from religion to science. Schools were invented to make obedient soldiers [Civ 45 · 16:44](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=1004), [Civ 45 · 29:36](https://youtu.be/DiRJyy-8V54?t=1776).
- **From "what is truth" to "how do we know truth"**: the question that created the scientific method. Once institutionalized, science "has become an imperial bureaucracy" [Civ 43 · 12:39](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=759), [Civ 43 · 52:18](https://youtu.be/CjtUnKcU1IA?t=3138).
- **Status versus wealth**: for most of history people turned money into status by feasting their community. Calvinism made hoarded wealth the goal, creating class and the middle-class pathologies of fearing disorder, germs and loss [Civ 49 · 22:01](https://youtu.be/JZ8P-FZHWnQ?t=1321), [Civ 49 · 1:00:27](https://youtu.be/JZ8P-FZHWnQ?t=3627).
- **Zombie civilization**: his label, via Weber, for a society that accumulates "a symbol" (money) with no spirit or purpose, heading toward "civilizational suicide" [Civ 42 · 20:40](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=1240), [Civ 42 · 1:10:25](https://youtu.be/TcxlOiJz8V0?t=4225).
- **Islam as proto-modernity**: Islam fused pagan intimacy with monotheist clarity and chose Aristotle over Plato. Europe then copied it through the Renaissance, Reformation and Scientific Revolution [Civ 37 · 40:40](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2440), [Civ 37 · 1:04:05](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=3845), [Civ 37 · 1:10:39](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=4239).
