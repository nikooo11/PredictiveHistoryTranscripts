# Chapter 1: Origins: The First Societies, Their Gods and Myths

*Mainly draws on Civilization #1–6 and #18–20 (Aug–Dec 2024) and Secret History #2, #6, #11–15 and #18 (Aug–Nov 2025), with Sargon details from Civilization #22, #23 and #39.*

## The story he tells

Jiang's account of human origins turns the textbook march of progress upside down. For most of our history, he argues, humans were peaceful, egalitarian, artistic and, above all, religious. Farming meant harder work and more disease, so people did not settle down for food. They settled around temples and charismatic shamans at Göbekli Tepe, Jericho and Çatalhöyük, worshipping a mother goddess who made all living things kin [Civ 1 · 48:37](https://youtu.be/Jjqf9T59uY0?t=2917). Émile Durkheim supplies the theory: religion is a society's shared imagination, the thing that makes it a society at all [Civ 2 · 46:13](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2773).

Then comes the fall, his "Paradise Lost." Following Marija Gimbutas, he describes a woman-led "Old Europe" overrun by the Yamnaya, steppe herders whose cattle economy produced private property, patriarchy and war, and whose sky father pushed aside the mother goddess [Civ 5 · 40:44](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=2444). Myth records the same inversion when the sky god Marduk kills the mother goddess Tiamat [SH 13 · 23:30](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1410).

In the river civilizations, religion becomes a tool of rule. Myths make hierarchy look like the will of heaven, and civilization becomes "a device meant to gaslight or fool people" [SH 13 · 07:10](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=430). Geography shapes each myth. Secure Egypt imagines generous gods and builds the Great Pyramid as its "Manhattan Project"; chaotic Mesopotamia serves violent gods and answers with the Epic of Gilgamesh; the trading cities of the Indus stay peaceful and, he speculates, "proto-Buddhist" [Civ 19 · 04:42](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=282). Sargon of Akkad, a usurping mercenary, builds the first empire.

Every hierarchy decays. Peter Turchin's "elite overproduction" explains, for him, why the Bronze Age world fell around 1200 BC and why all societies eventually fall [Civ 6 · 29:44](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1784). From the corrupt late Bronze Age comes Zarathustra, whom he calls the most influential person who ever lived, the source of the monotheism behind Judaism, Christianity and Islam [SH 18 · 15:17](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=917). These patterns (religion as social glue, overbred elites, steppe outsiders, myth as propaganda) are the tools he later turns on Rome, the Church and today's America. The 2025 Secret History lectures retell the 2024 Civilization story with sharper edges: civilization as deception, and the steppe peoples as the real innovators.

## The main figures

### Émile Durkheim

*Who:* French sociologist (1858–1917), a founder of modern sociology, who argued in *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) that religion is how a society represents itself to its members.

**What he teaches**
- He brings in Durkheim ("Emil Duram" in the captions), "considered the founder of Sociology," to clinch his claim that religion is what makes us human, right after presenting Ice Age cave art as ritual [Civ 2 · 42:15](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2535).
- Durkheim defines religion as ideas by which people "imagine the Society" they belong to. Jiang's gloss: "it's to create society" [Civ 2 · 43:16](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2596).
- Religion was humanity's first theory of how things connect, so philosophy and science grew out of it, and "science today is our religion" [Civ 2 · 45:18](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2718).
- Paired with Kant (reality is built by the mind), religion becomes a society's collective thought: no society without religion, and no religion without society [Civ 2 · 46:13](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2773).

**In his big picture:** This underpins his method. Every society, ours included ("today our mythology would be Science and History"), runs on shared myths, so you read a civilization through its myths [Civ 19 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=0).

**In his words:** "religion is our Collective Consciousness it's what every it's what allows Society to come into being" [Civ 2 · 46:13](https://youtu.be/x1E5rRmCiT4?t=2773)

### Marija Gimbutas

*Who:* Lithuanian-born American archaeologist (1921–1994), at Harvard and UCLA, who proposed the "Kurgan hypothesis" (Indo-European languages spread from the Pontic steppe) and described a goddess-centred Neolithic "Old Europe."

**What he teaches**
- In her account, Old Europe (about 6500–2500 BC), settled by Anatolian farmers carrying the mother-goddess religion, was ruled by women and therefore peaceful, egalitarian and artistic [Civ 4 · 29:07](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1747).
- Her Kurgan hypothesis rests on graves: farmers buried together without weapons, steppe newcomers buried alone with weapons, cattle and horses. He reads this as property-owning, male-led warriors replacing egalitarian farmers [Civ 4 · 26:11](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1571).
- She "was laughed at," he says, because male professors could not accept women in charge, and because people assume humans are always violent and driven by money [Civ 4 · 27:05](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1625). DNA, he says, now shows the conquest was "a genocide" and has "proven True" her idea [Civ 4 · 18:54](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1134), [29:07](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1747).
- From her books he draws Old Europe's inverted values: weapons they chose not to make, the snake as a symbol of life, black for fertility and white for death, and writing invented for religion rather than trade [Civ 4 · 31:44](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1904), [34:43](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=2083).
- With Christopher Ryan's *Sex at Dawn* he adds that women had several partners, which bound groups together, and that women rule better because they cooperate and control men through gossip, shame and sex rather than violence [Civ 4 · 52:37](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=3157). He repeats all this in 2025 [SH 14 · 35:20](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=2120).

**In his big picture:** Gimbutas supplies his "Paradise Lost," the peaceful, woman-led baseline against which he measures every later civilization.

**In his words:** "Europe was ruled by woman and because of that Europe was peaceful egalitarian and artistic" [Civ 4 · 29:07](https://youtu.be/RaT_ZUDjHrM?t=1747)

**Reality check:** Ancient DNA does show a large, male-biased steppe migration into Europe around 3000–2500 BC, which supports her steppe origin for Indo-European languages. It cannot show that Old Europe was ruled by women, and most archaeologists reject her matriarchal reading.

### The Yamnaya

*Who:* A herding culture of the Pontic-Caspian steppe (about 3300–2600 BC), widely linked with the spread of Proto-Indo-European languages.

**What he teaches**
- Scarce grassland forced "social evolution," which he defines as "open Cooperative competition": rival groups trade and compete, and the outsider who adopts every innovation most ruthlessly wins [Civ 5 · 05:43](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=343), [10:00](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=600).
- Their innovations were herding, lactose tolerance (making them, he says, "20 cm" taller than farmers), the tamed horse and the wheel, which together gave wagon-borne nomadism [Civ 5 · 14:57](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=897), [16:59](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1019).
- Cattle are wealth that can be stolen, so herding produced private property, patriarchy and primogeniture: the eldest son inherits, and his brothers must raid, which creates a war culture [Civ 5 · 18:59](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1139), [21:23](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1283). Their god became a "sky father," the source of Zeus and Jupiter, who gives cattle and demands fighting [Civ 5 · 22:21](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1341).
- Europe's more numerous farmers were weakened by plague and a cold spell, and bands of young men killed the local men and married the women [Civ 5 · 26:34](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1594), [30:36](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1836). In 2025 he adds that Stonehenge's builders vanished, and that India got caste instead of genocide [SH 14 · 45:13](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=2713), [46:02](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=2762).
- The Himalayas kept them out of China, so the Indo-European world from Europe to India became "the West," while China stayed apart [Civ 5 · 32:48](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=1968).

**In his big picture:** The Yamnaya bring patriarchy, money and war into the world. Their heirs include the Greeks, Romans, Persians and Indo-Aryans, and, he agrees when a student asks, the Mongols [Civ 5 · 33:46](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=2026).

**In his words:** "now with the Amaya [Yamnaya] you have patriarchy you have War you have money" [Civ 5 · 40:44](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=2444)

**Reality check:** The male-biased steppe migration is well supported, but the Mongols' ancestry is overwhelmingly Northeast Asian rather than Yamnaya, and a 20 cm average height gap is not established.

### The steppe peoples

*Who:* The successive horse-herding peoples of the Eurasian grasslands, from the Scythians through the Xiongnu, Huns and Turks to the Mongols.

**What he teaches**
- He inverts the school contrast between civilization and barbarians: the steppe peoples are the open, curious innovators, while empires grow closed and static [SH 14 · 03:37](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=217).
- His cycle: competing states innovate; a poor periphery like the Qin unifies them; the empire hardens into a bureaucracy; its surplus elite splits and hires steppe mercenaries, who then take over [SH 14 · 07:06](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=426), [13:26](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=806).
- Steppe society runs on patron–client loyalty, "like a mafia," not debt slavery, so every warrior fights as a free man. Oath-bound gangs of landless sons raid for cattle and women, and "the Vikings are the direct descendants" of these brotherhoods [SH 14 · 25:10](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=1510), [53:29](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=3209).
- Their founding myth, a first man sacrificing his twin, recurs in Romulus and in Genghis Khan killing his best friend, marking "a culture based on violence and exploitation" [SH 14 · 48:00](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=2880).
- Waves follow: Scythians and Medes; the Xiongnu ("the Huns"), pushed west by Han China toward Rome; then Turks, Mongols and Timur, until gunpowder ends steppe power [SH 14 · 55:28](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=3328), [56:56](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=3416).

**In his big picture:** The steppe is the outside force in his model of imperial decline, seen more admiringly in 2025 than in 2024. Genghis Khan and the Mongols are covered in Chapter 8.

**In his words:** "the steps are like a training ground for fighters and over time the fighters just become more and more fierce" [SH 14 · 28:05](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=1685)

### Khufu

*Who:* Fourth-Dynasty Egyptian pharaoh (reigned about 2589–2566 BC) for whom the Great Pyramid of Giza was built.

**What he teaches**
- Khufu ("curfew" in the captions) built the Great Pyramid about 2500 BC over some 26 years. Its capstone, the benben, recalls the mound where the creator god Atum-Ra first appeared [Civ 18 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=0), [01:13](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=73).
- He rejects the tomb theory: no body has been found in any of about 100 pyramids, divine benefactor-kings would not spend their reigns on a selfish tomb, and a pharaoh might die before it was finished [Civ 18 · 07:56](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=476), [09:56](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=596).
- His theory: the pyramid was "Egypt's Manhattan Project," a machine that channelled the dead pharaoh's divine energy, through his mummy and the worshippers in the Grand Gallery, to unite Egypt, awe its enemies and master the Nile [Civ 18 · 14:43](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=883), [16:54](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=1014), [21:34](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=1294).
- Building stopped because the "4.2 kiloyear" drought (about 2200 BC) broke faith in the pharaoh, the centrally planned "pyramid economy" bred inequality, corruption and waste, and a death-obsessed religion squandered wealth on grave goods [Civ 18 · 31:40](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=1900), [32:43](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=1963).
- The mummies are missing because the pharaoh's cult hid them, since whoever held the body would "control God"; and the Tower of Babel is the Israelites mocking the pyramid [Civ 18 · 45:43](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=2743), [38:58](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=2338).

**In his big picture:** The pyramid shows what pre-literate, pre-scientific minds could imagine, and so "shows the limitations of our own imagination" [Civ 18 · 53:29](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=3209).

**In his words:** "the Egyptians conceptualized and created the Great Pyramid in order to harness the power of God in order to create Eternal peace on Earth" [Civ 18 · 15:48](https://youtu.be/KSSSCznYrGo?t=948)

**Reality check:** He calls his theory speculation. Egyptologists overwhelmingly see the pyramids as royal tombs and blame the missing bodies on ancient tomb robbers.

### Ra, Osiris, Isis, Horus and Set

*Who:* Central gods of Egyptian religion: Ra the sun god; Osiris, murdered by his brother Set and restored by his wife Isis; and their son Horus, whose earthly form was the reigning pharaoh.

**What he teaches**
- His retelling: Ra hands the throne to Osiris, who gives Egypt cities, pyramids and writing. Jealous Set lures Osiris into a "comfortable" sarcophagus and dismembers him, and Isis reassembles him and conceives Horus [Civ 19 · 23:42](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1422), [24:37](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1477).
- Horus and Set duel (once as hippopotamuses, when a furious Horus beheads Isis for failing to spear Set). Horus wins and founds kingship, so every pharaoh is his descendant or reincarnation [Civ 19 · 25:35](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1535), [26:37](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1597).
- Protected by desert and sea and fed by a predictable Nile, Egypt imagined benevolent gods who give everything, and prized cleverness over bravery, the reverse of Mesopotamia [Civ 19 · 29:54](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1794), [30:58](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1858).
- In 2025 he reads the myth as a script. Ra (the "virtuous hero"), Osiris (the "passive victim") and Horus (the "vengeanceful child") were identities that priests, he speculates, drilled into drugged pharaohs through traumatic re-enactments, with a priest in an Isis mask as the pharaoh's controller [SH 6 · 19:44](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=1184), [21:20](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=1280). The priests were "what we today call the deep state" [SH 6 · 27:22](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=1642).
- He stresses "this is all speculation" [SH 6 · 25:28](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=1528), yet says the techniques are still used, linking them to CIA interrogation and hinting that the name of ISIS is no coincidence [SH 6 · 37:52](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=2272) (see Chapter 10).

**In his big picture:** Egypt is his model of a stable, centralized empire whose myth sanctifies the king, and, from 2025, his earliest case of priests secretly steering a ruler.

**In his words:** "It's not something to be believed. It's something to be acted out." [SH 6 · 16:52](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=1012)

**Reality check:** Pharaohs were ritually identified with Horus and Osiris, but there is no evidence that priests trauma-conditioned them. The idea mirrors modern "mind-control programming" conspiracy theories, which psychiatry does not support.

### Tiamat and Marduk

*Who:* In the Babylonian creation epic *Enuma Elish* (2nd millennium BC), Tiamat is the primeval salt-water goddess, and Marduk, Babylon's patron god, is the young god who kills her and makes the world from her body.

**What he teaches**
- Apsu (fresh water) and Tiamat (salt water) beget the gods. Apsu plots to kill his noisy children and is killed; Tiamat raises an army; the gods elect Marduk, who kills her, makes sky and earth from her body and creates humans as the gods' slaves [Civ 19 · 27:48](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1668), [28:58](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1738), from the blood of the rebel Kingu [SH 13 · 30:37](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1837).
- He reads it as layered history. Tiamat is farming society's mother goddess, and her defeat is the egalitarian order giving way to urban patriarchy, justified as order taming chaos (the flooding river, a water serpent like the Chinese dragon) [Civ 19 · 46:12](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2772), [47:18](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2838).
- It teaches bravery, servitude to the gods and "Creative destruction": to build the new you must destroy the old [Civ 19 · 32:12](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=1932).
- In 2025 he calls it propaganda. Marduk's calendar and ranks of gods make bureaucracy "divinely ordained," and Babylon becomes the gods' own city, which no one should leave: "This is the mandate of heaven" [SH 13 · 28:34](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1714), [33:02](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1982). The Anunnaki-as-aliens theory is "a really stupid idea" [SH 13 · 14:18](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=858).

**In his big picture:** Marduk killing Tiamat is his central example of "inversion," each new order dethroning the old and rewriting the myth [SH 13 · 17:43](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1063).

**In his words:** "not only is monarch [Marduk] proclaiming a new order but he's also proclaiming new values. New values of struggle, exploitation, toil." [SH 13 · 27:59](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1679)

### Gilgamesh

*Who:* Legendary king of Uruk (probably a real ruler around 2700 BC) and hero of the *Epic of Gilgamesh*, one of the oldest surviving works of literature.

**What he teaches**
- A giant demigod king of Uruk, "the first city," bullies young men and rapes young women, so the gods make Enkidu from clay. The two fight, become best friends, spurn Ishtar and kill the Bull of Heaven, and the gods kill Enkidu [Civ 19 · 36:53](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2213), [38:01](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2281).
- Terrified of death, Gilgamesh seeks the immortal flood survivor (Utnapishtim, unnamed in his telling), whose story is "the basis" of Noah's. He fails, goes home, and on seeing Uruk's walls and happy people understands what immortality is [Civ 19 · 39:56](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2396), [41:52](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2512).
- The epic is Mesopotamia's reply to Egypt, written "in a dialectic with the pyramids": immortality in stone is an illusion, and a ruler lives on by caring for his people [Civ 19 · 34:40](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2080), [44:04](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2644).
- It records a shift from heroic king to bureaucracy, as the king learns humility [Civ 19 · 49:41](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2981).
- In 2025 he calls Gilgamesh "a bureaucratic creation": local hero legends merge as kingdoms merge, then priests add morals to control the king, like a campus legend that grows and then gets moralized [SH 13 · 36:29](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=2189), [41:04](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=2464).

**In his big picture:** Gilgamesh marks the birth of literature, stories "implanted in the minds of others for centuries" [Civ 19 · 42:56](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2576), a thread he follows through the Bible, Homer and Dante (Chapters 2, 3 and 6).

**In his words:** "immortality is not living forever immortality is to be remembered by the people who love you" [Civ 19 · 41:52](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=2512)

### Sargon of Akkad

*Who:* Founder of the Akkadian Empire (reigned about 2334–2279 BC), who united the Sumerian city-states; often called the first empire-builder.

**What he teaches**
- After long, indecisive wars among Sumer's city-states, the outsider Akkadians adopted all their innovations and conquered them [Civ 5 · 12:04](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=724).
- Sargon was a mercenary and cupbearer to the king of Kish (probably the king's lover, Jiang speculates) who killed him and took the throne [Civ 39 · 32:27](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=1947). He won legitimacy by destroying Lugalzagesi, the temple-burning warlord, parading him and killing him [Civ 23 · 23:43](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=1423).
- His name means "legitimate king," which tells you no one thought he was. The story of a low-born foreign mercenary who overthrows his master recurs with Zeus, Romulus, King David and Genghis Khan [Civ 23 · 22:45](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=1365), [SH 13 · 45:42](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=2742).
- He created the first professional army and perfected siege warfare [Civ 39 · 37:29](https://youtu.be/mPQzAPnjYkM?t=2249), [Civ 44 · 50:37](https://youtu.be/-DnfGcvZrfA?t=3037), but the first empire unleashed "a cycle of violence," an arms race of fear [Civ 23 · 23:43](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=1423).
- His empire fell to the 4.2 kiloyear climate crisis [Civ 20 · 28:47](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1727). His daughter Enheduanna, high priestess and "the first named author in human history," is Jiang's precedent for a royal woman writing the Bible's Yahwist source [Civ 22 · 32:29](https://youtu.be/GhLd_h1QsL4?t=1949) (see Chapter 2).

**In his big picture:** Sargon is the archetypal usurper-founder in a pattern Jiang traces from Akkad to the Mongols.

**In his words:** "Sargon his name means legitimate King by the way okay legitimate King which tells us no one thought he was legitimate" [Civ 23 · 22:45](https://youtu.be/HvVTNTPzq7E?t=1365)

**Reality check:** Sargon's early life survives mainly in later legend, and nothing suggests he was the king's lover. Jiang's date of "about 2000 BC" is a few centuries late.

### The Harappans (Indus Valley civilization)

*Who:* The Bronze Age civilization of the Indus Valley (at its height about 2600–1900 BC), in today's Pakistan and northwest India; its script is undeciphered.

**What he teaches**
- Bigger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined (about 5 million people), it lived by long-distance trade yet shows no sign of organized warfare: no warrior caste, armour or weapons of war [Civ 20 · 06:42](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=402), [09:01](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=541).
- It was relatively equal: no palaces or temples, walls for customs rather than defence, private seat toilets, wind-tower "air conditioners," standard bricks and weights, and half the people living past 55 [Civ 20 · 12:24](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=744), [14:20](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=860), [15:17](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=917).
- His explanation is a "dialectic": traders disgusted by Sumer's wars and Egypt's wasteful pyramids chose the opposite, as Canada defines itself against the United States ("Canadians invented peacekeeping") [Civ 20 · 20:59](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1259), [22:44](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1364).
- It declined with the 4.2 kiloyear drought, collapsing trade and elite overproduction. Steppe newcomers became Proto-Indo-Iranians (whose soma, fire rites and horse sacrifice fed Zoroastrianism) and pushed into India, where taking local women and killing or enslaving local men produced the castes [Civ 20 · 30:52](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1852), [34:45](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2085), [37:44](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2264).
- His self-described "very strange argument": karma, dharma, reincarnation and the world as false reality have no Indo-European source, so the Indus religion must have been proto-Buddhism, surviving in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism as nostalgia for animist oneness [Civ 20 · 52:22](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=3142), [57:20](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=3440).

**In his big picture:** The Indus proves that civilization need not mean hierarchy and war, the "exception to the rule" [SH 14 · 09:31](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=571); its longing for oneness echoes in Plato's cave and the Second Coming [Civ 20 · 56:23](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=3383).

**In his words:** "I would bet that if we were able to construct the religion of the IVC the portal religion we would see a Proto Buddhism" [Civ 20 · 54:16](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=3256)

**Reality check:** He admits most scholars would reject the proto-Buddhism idea. The "peaceful, egalitarian" picture is itself debated, and Sikhism, which he groups with these religions, began in the 15th century AD.

### The Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama)

*Who:* Spiritual teacher of about the 5th century BC from the Himalayan foothills, founder of Buddhism.

**What he teaches**
- Buddhism was a class revolt. Brahmin priests (the "upper nobility") controlled salvation and outranked kings (the "lower nobility"); the Buddha "was actually a prince," and his path reached nirvana without Brahmins [Civ 20 · 47:56](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2876), [49:00](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2940).
- Kings spread it. Ashoka made it a state religion and sent missions to China, Greece and Egypt [Civ 20 · 47:56](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2876); in 2026 Jiang describes him turning to Buddhism after winning all his wars [Meet-Up 4 · 20:13](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=1213).
- The Brahmins struck back with the caste system and by absorbing yoga, meditation and nirvana, until Hinduism won in India [Civ 20 · 50:10](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=3010).
- He ranks the Buddha with Jesus, Muhammad and Confucius among the charismatic visionaries who found religions [Civ 1 · 23:22](https://youtu.be/Jjqf9T59uY0?t=1402).

**In his big picture:** The Buddha's teaching is the Indus "proto-Buddhism" resurfacing, used by kings against priests. Later he contrasts it with Zarathustra's insistence that compassion is not enough [SH 18 · 40:33](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2433).

**In his words:** "Buddhism is in many respects a revolutionary religion to overthrow the power of the Brahman class" [Civ 20 · 49:00](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=2940)

**Reality check:** The four-class varna order already appears in the Rigveda, centuries before the Buddha, so caste did not begin as a response to him.

### Peter Turchin

*Who:* Russian-born American scientist (born 1957), founder of "cliodynamics," the mathematical modelling of history, and author of *War and Peace and War* and *End Times*.

**What he teaches**
- Turchin corrects Marx: societies collapse not because the poor revolt but because the elite fight among themselves [Civ 6 · 15:17](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=917), [16:33](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=993).
- A permanent hereditary elite brings organization, wealth and scale, but it lives by "rent seeking behavior," like a landlord or a university selling degrees, kept bearable by a social contract of feasts and temples [Civ 6 · 20:44](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1244), [21:45](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1305).
- Rent breeds debt slavery below and too many would-be landlords above. The surplus can only be absorbed by redistribution, civil war or conquest, so "unless you can control ... the number of the elite in your Society your Society must collapse" [Civ 6 · 29:44](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1784), [32:28](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1948).
- He applies this to the Bronze Age collapse, against the scholarly "perfect storm" consensus: Mycenaean palaces kept taking their cut in bad years, making societies brittle enough to fall to any shock [Civ 6 · 39:46](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=2386), [43:06](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=2586).
- In 2025 he adds John Calhoun's "rat utopia": trapped rats fight to the death, like "the children of the elite," who form factions, hire mercenaries and bring civil war or revolution [SH 2 · 20:38](https://youtu.be/K-_l9jBGo74?t=1238), [38:37](https://youtu.be/K-_l9jBGo74?t=2317).

**In his big picture:** Elite overproduction is one of his master keys, used for the Bronze Age, the Indus, Rome and today's West. He even calls collapse healthy, like a forest fire [Civ 6 · 46:01](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=2761).

**In his words:** "the problem with Society isn't that you there's too many poor people the problem are the problem is there are too many rich people" [Civ 6 · 16:33](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=993)

**Reality check:** Turchin's model also stresses popular immiseration and state fiscal crisis, and its predictive claims are disputed. Most specialists see the Late Bronze Age collapse as a multi-cause "systems collapse."

### Zarathustra (Zoroaster)

*Who:* Iranian priest-prophet (dates disputed, often placed around 1500–1000 BC) whose hymns, the Gathas, founded Zoroastrianism, later the religion of the Persian empires.

**What he teaches**
- He introduces him as "the most influential person who has ever lived," whose religion led to the first great empire, Persia [SH 18 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=0).
- The setting is the late Bronze Age, "the height of capital," war and slavery, in a poor mining periphery of Iran that he likens to Congo. A priest himself, Zarathustra was disgusted by priests who sold immortality [SH 18 · 20:58](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=1258), [22:20](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=1340).
- He set one supreme god, Ahura Mazda, "the lord of wisdom," symbolized by fire, above self-serving gods, and moved the war of heaven and hell inside each person: asha (truth, virtue) against druj (the lie) [SH 18 · 08:10](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=490), [08:57](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=537).
- Read through Kant's categorical imperative, asha yields three revolutionary ideas (the individual, free choice and truth) that make monotheism: "Zoroism will give birth to three new religions ... Judaism, Christianity, and Islam" [SH 18 · 10:07](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=607), [15:06](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=906).
- The Gathas teach "good thoughts, good words and good actions" and a final judgment faced alone [SH 18 · 25:40](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=1540). Evil exists so that virtue can be won, and unlike Buddhism, "justice is about action" [SH 18 · 38:51](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2331), [40:45](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2445).
- He calls Nietzsche and Rumi his reincarnations [SH 18 · 35:47](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2147), and tells a student that Ahura Mazda is complete forgiveness: "Hitler will be forgiven because everyone will be forgiven," and "there is no hell" [SH 18 · 58:37](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=3517), [58:54](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=3534).

**In his big picture:** Zarathustra is his first "poet prophet" answering a corrupt order, like Homer and the Yahwist [SH 18 · 07:07](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=427), and a source for Plato's cave [SH 18 · 15:47](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=947). Nietzsche is covered in Chapter 9.

**In his words:** "that's why I say Zorra Thusra is the most important individual who's ever lived because he will create the structure for montheism" [SH 18 · 15:17](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=917)

**Reality check:** Scholars debate how far Zoroastrian ideas shaped later Judaism, Christianity and Islam, but none is generally seen as its offspring. The Gathas he reads also promise "long punishment" for followers of the lie.

## Key ideas from these lectures

- **Religion came first**: people settled around temples and charismatic leaders, and farming followed to sustain the temples [Civ 1 · 48:37](https://youtu.be/Jjqf9T59uY0?t=2917), [SH 13 · 04:10](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=250).
- **The religious imagination**: a belief shared by a whole community is "more real than reality," so disbelief is the gravest crime [Civ 3 · 46:22](https://youtu.be/VanPH0GFTsA?t=2782).
- **Mythology as operating system**: myth is a society's shared reality, "the operating system of the culture" [Civ 20 · 18:09](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1089).
- **Mother goddess to sky god**: farmers worshipped a life-giving mother who asks for harmony; warrior societies worship a sky god (Ra, Marduk, Zeus, Jupiter) who demands struggle and conquest [SH 13 · 18:17](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1097).
- **Inversion**: "history is a constant process of inversion," as each new order (sky god over mother goddess, son over father, bureaucrat over king) dethrones the old [SH 13 · 17:43](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1063).
- **Open cooperative competition**: rivals who compete but keep trading innovate fastest, until the most ruthless adopter conquers the rest [Civ 5 · 10:00](https://youtu.be/J4HTFJWl5D8?t=600), [SH 14 · 05:22](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=322).
- **Patriarchy, money and war**: "These three things always go together," born together on the steppe with primogeniture [SH 14 · 19:24](https://youtu.be/JpTI2gFYWrU?t=1164).
- **Mandate of heaven**: his general term for myth that makes a hierarchy look divinely ordained, used for Babylon and Egypt [SH 13 · 33:02](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=1982), China's dynastic cycle [Civ 31 · 09:51](https://youtu.be/HIoYQBbBllk?t=591) and Zeus's plan in the *Iliad* [GB 4 · 33:41](https://youtu.be/dkzr5A8IlLA?t=2021).
- **Civilization as gaslighting**: humans could always make art, religion and science; "civilization" is the device that makes an unjust hierarchy seem legitimate, which is why kings' myths favour docile farmers over free herders (the Sumerian *Debate between Sheep and Grain*) [SH 13 · 07:10](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=430), [52:24](https://youtu.be/FJF8oD_5c_w?t=3144).
- **Geography is destiny**: borders and rivers shape each people's gods and values, as with benevolent Egypt against embattled Mesopotamia [Civ 19 · 50:49](https://youtu.be/e92jyBMmAyM?t=3049).
- **Kiloyear events**: abrupt climate shifts force migrations and topple regimes; the 4.2 event ended Egypt's Old Kingdom, Akkad and the Indus cities [Civ 20 · 28:47](https://youtu.be/cvI8rukoda8?t=1727).
- **Elite overproduction and rent-seeking**: elites earn by charging rent, multiply faster than positions, fight, and bring the society down [Civ 6 · 20:44](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1244), [29:44](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1784).
- **Capital as monetized power**: the Bronze Age tin trade was "proto-capitalist," and "capital is just the monetization of power," which exploits, alienates and consolidates, so it rises fast and crashes fast [SH 15 · 07:52](https://youtu.be/rkt0HQOogHE?t=472), [42:48](https://youtu.be/rkt0HQOogHE?t=2568).
- **Secret societies at the first temples**: he claims oath-bound factions fought over the first temples and the losers were sacrificed, reading human remains at Göbekli Tepe this way [SH 12 · 25:39](https://youtu.be/VtnRA-GNKM8?t=1539), [26:01](https://youtu.be/VtnRA-GNKM8?t=1561). Excavators have found worked human skull fragments there, but no evidence of a sacrificed faction.
- **Asha**: Zarathustra's truth-and-virtue, which Jiang says is always changing, different for everyone and never finished [SH 18 · 51:04](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=3064).
