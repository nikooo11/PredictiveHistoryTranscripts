# Chapter 5: Jesus, the Church, and Islam

*Draws mainly on Civilization #24–28, #33, #34, #37 and #40 (Dec 2024–Mar 2025), Secret History #22–24 (Nov–Dec 2025) and the June 2026 Dante sessions, with later remarks from Great Books, Geo-Strategy Update #7 and the 2026 Global Meet-Ups.*

## The story he tells

For Jiang, the history of Christianity is the story of a liberating idea captured by an empire. Jesus, a Galilean "religious genius", taught what every "poet prophet" teaches: each of us carries a divine spark from a true God, and love, forgiveness and indifference to wealth make it glow ([Civ 24 · 38:20](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2300), [SH 22 · 22:10](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1330)). Rome crucified him as a rebel against its slave order ([SH 22 · 28:31](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1711)).

Paul, a wealthy Hellenized Jew whom Jiang suspects of being an imperial agent, then turned a religion *by* Jesus into a religion *about* Jesus: faith in a saviour, obedience, and a church modelled on the Roman patriarchal household ([Civ 25 · 10:01](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=601), [SH 23 · 02:48](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=168)). Jesus's own movement, led by his brother James, was destroyed in the Jewish wars, and its remnants drifted toward Arabia ([Civ 26 · 05:57](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=357)). Constantine adopted Paul's church to unify his empire, and the Trinity fixed at Nicaea made God an unarguable "equation", which Jiang treats as the birth of monotheism and, in the long run, of modern money, science and the nation-state ([Civ 26 · 29:58](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1798)). Augustine then took the Church "out of history" and taught that humans are born evil and should simply obey, which Jiang blames for the Dark Ages ([Civ 27 · 24:32](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=1472)).

Dissenters fled east, where Muhammad united Arabs, Jews and heretical Christians in what Jiang calls "the world's first Global Revolution" ([Civ 28 · 20:17](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=1217)). Byzantium enforced the Trinity by the sword, and Rome allied with Charlemagne to create the "useful fiction" of the Holy Roman Empire ([Civ 34 · 56:18](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=3378)). The medieval Church held a "faith monopoly", defended by scapegoating Jews, crusading against Muslims and Cathars, and absorbing reformers like Francis and Dominic ([Civ 40 · 19:34](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1174)). Aquinas tried to update Augustine; in Jiang's telling only Dante's poetry finally freed Europe ([Civ 41 · 49:54](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=2994)).

The tone sharpens over time. The December 2024 Civilization lectures read as comparative religious history; by Secret History (late 2025) the Catholic Church "really is the church of Satan" ([SH 22 · 1:07:51](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=4071)); in the 2026 Dante sessions Jesus came to liberate us "from power itself" ([Dante 5 · 12:17](https://youtu.be/xPxEi5Zm0G4?t=737)).

## The main figures

### Jesus of Nazareth

*Who:* Jewish preacher and healer from Galilee (c. 4 BCE – c. 30–33 CE), crucified by the Romans under Pontius Pilate. Christians worship him as the Son of God; Muslims honour him as a prophet.

**What he teaches**
- **Two Jesuses.** He separates the few facts "most Scholars agree" on (born in cosmopolitan Galilee, pupil of John the Baptist, healer, crucified by Rome) from the Gospel story, whose puzzles (how can the Son of God die, and why must he?) he thinks Christianity never solved ([Civ 24 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=0), [Civ 24 · 08:57](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=537)). He admits "I'm not a scholar" ([Civ 24 · 25:08](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1508)).
- **A synthesizer with three layers.** At the world's crossroads, Jesus fused Plato, Zoroastrianism, Hindu and Buddhist ideas and Cynicism into what we now call Gnosticism (captioned "narcissism") ([Civ 24 · 38:20](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2300)). He preached compassion to the public, self-sacrifice to disciples, and secretly that this world is false and a divine spark lets us escape it ([Civ 24 · 39:20](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2360)).
- **Thomas as his true voice.** The Gospel of Thomas, found at Nag Hammadi in 1945, is his key source, trusted because it is poetry, agrees with other "poet prophets", and matches what Dante and Dostoevsky later saw ([SH 22 · 1:27:59](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=5279)).
- **Why he mattered.** Rome was "particularly evil", so Jesus explained why evil wins: this world is "a corpse", and the powerful are like Achilles after killing Hector, haunted and pitiable. The oppressed should forgive them, as Priam did ([SH 22 · 23:28](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1408), [SH 22 · 27:00](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1620)).
- **Why Rome killed him.** Crucifixion was for bandits and rebels ([SH 22 · 07:40](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=460)); telling slaves they were free made Jesus a thief of Roman property ([SH 23 · 01:36](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=96)). It backfired: when a king kills a critic, people assume the critic told the truth ([Civ 24 · 52:34](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=3154)).
- **Against the institution.** He reads "Lord, Lord" (Matthew 7) as Jesus saying "Don't use my name" ([SH 22 · 1:34:27](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=5667)); in 2026 Jesus is "first and foremost a poet" whose words spread like "a mind virus" ([Meet-Up 4 · 2:16:28](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=8188), [Meet-Up 4 · 2:16:45](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=8205)).

**In his big picture:** Jesus is the model "poet prophet" and the touchstone for his contrast between the divine spark and organized religion, empire and money ("Satan is money" [SH 22 · 1:33:01](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=5581)). The crucifixion is also a story template, "a re-imagining of Plato's allegory of the cave" ([GSU 5 · 21:15](https://youtu.be/5I2VPYPJJ68?t=1275)), and a Passion pattern he maps onto Robespierre ([Civ 47 · 31:13](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1873)).

**In his words:** "So Jesus is really about the democratization of the divine." [SH 22 · 31:41](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1901)

**Reality check:** Most scholars date the Gospel of Thomas and developed Gnostic systems to the second century and see the historical Jesus as a Jewish apocalyptic preacher; a Gnostic, Buddhist-influenced Jesus is a speculative minority view. That Rome executed him is standard history.

### John the Baptist

*Who:* Jewish prophet who preached repentance and baptized followers in the Jordan around 28–30 CE; the Gospels cast him as Jesus's forerunner. Herod Antipas had him executed.

**What he teaches**
- Jesus's teacher, a "major Jewish religious leader" who used baptism to cleanse sin before the coming Kingdom of Heaven; Jesus then built his own following with a different message ([Civ 24 · 01:05](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=65)).
- An "apocalyptic preacher": God is coming to destroy the wicked, so repent now. Jiang counts John's teaching of Jesus among the few facts "mainstream academics" accept ([SH 22 · 05:22](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=322)). He thinks John was probably an Essene ([SH 22 · 15:59](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=959)).
- In 2026 John becomes a biblical type: the long-awaited son sent "to announce the coming of the Messiah", which Jiang reads into Mark Zuckerberg's family story as "coded language" ([Meet-Up 2 · 1:00:22](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=3622)).

**In his big picture:** John shows that Jesus came from a crowded field of end-times prophets and "was not unique in his message that the kingdom of heaven was returning" ([Civ 24 · 31:41](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1901)).

**In his words:** "That is the message of John the Baptist and Jesus was one of his students, one of his disciples." [SH 22 · 05:41](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=341)

**Reality check:** A link between John and the Essenes is possible but unproven.

### Mary, mother of Jesus

*Who:* Mother of Jesus, venerated in Catholic and Orthodox Christianity as the Virgin Mary and Mother of God, and honoured in the Quran.

**What he teaches**
- A puzzle for Christology: if Jesus is divine, how could a human woman bear him, and doesn't that make her divine? "So, who is Mary and what is her power?" ([SH 24 · 13:54](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=834), [SH 24 · 14:02](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=842)).
- In Dante's heaven she, not Jesus, is at the centre of the host, since a mortal woman giving birth to God may be a greater miracle than God dying ([Civ 29 · 05:12](https://youtu.be/GfqJi0qevok?t=312)); Bernard channels her love so Dante can see God ([Civ 29 · 16:18](https://youtu.be/GfqJi0qevok?t=978)).
- She answers Augustine: if all are born in sin, how could Mary bear God? In Jiang's reading of Dante, a mother's love cleansed her, because true love cannot contain sin ([Civ 29 · 17:22](https://youtu.be/GfqJi0qevok?t=1042)).
- The Quran's "Messiah son of Mary" is only a messenger and his mother "a woman of truth": neither is divine ([SH 24 · 53:13](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=3193)).

**In his big picture:** Mary is where he locates both the paradoxes of official doctrine and Dante's counter-theology of love.

**In his words:** "in Dante's rendering of Christian theology Mary is at the center" [Civ 29 · 05:12](https://youtu.be/GfqJi0qevok?t=312)

### Pontius Pilate

*Who:* Roman prefect of Judea (26–36 CE) who sentenced Jesus to crucifixion.

**What he teaches**
- The Gospels make Pilate "very reluctant and even at times sympathetic", a pro-Roman slant; the real Pilate was "a violent Thug" who pushed the emperor's cult into Jerusalem ([Civ 24 · 22:22](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1342), [Civ 24 · 23:22](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1402)).
- When Jews bared their necks rather than accept the emperor's images, Pilate had to back down and lost face ([Civ 24 · 24:13](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1453)). So he crucified Jesus "as basically a middle finger to the Jewish people", accidentally creating a martyr ([Civ 26 · 05:13](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=313)).
- John's Passion, with Pilate finding "no case" but yielding to the crowd, shows "the Bible is not that reliable", because it shifts blame onto the Jews ([SH 23 · 26:15](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=1575)).

**In his big picture:** Pilate anchors his claim that the Romans, not the Jews, killed Jesus, and that the Gospel story excusing Rome was written for Rome ([SH 22 · 29:47](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1787)).

**In his words:** "if P pilot could kill a Jew he would kill a Jew" [Civ 24 · 24:13](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1453)

**Reality check:** Philo and Josephus do describe a harsh Pilate and the images episode; the "middle finger" motive is Jiang's inference.

### Judas Iscariot

*Who:* One of the Twelve Apostles; in the Gospels he betrays Jesus to the Temple authorities for silver.

**What he teaches**
- In the Bible story Judas reports Jesus to the priests, who hand him to Pilate ([Civ 24 · 06:01](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=361)).
- In Leonardo's *Last Supper* Judas sits in shadow, turned from the light, clutching coins, his neck rigid ([Civ 41 · 26:18](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1578)); Jesus has already forgiven him, an idea Jiang traces to Dante ([Civ 41 · 30:10](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1810)).
- Dante's Satan chews Judas along with Brutus and Cassius ([GB 10 · 32:05](https://youtu.be/wGpdMYa2bME?t=1925)). The paradox: if God's plan needed Caesar's murder (leading to Augustus) and Jesus's betrayal (leading to the cross), why punish the betrayers? Since Dante insists on free will, predestination "can't be the answer" ([Dante 9 · 1:46:39](https://youtu.be/SSmJKnUOsAc?t=6399), [Dante 9 · 1:48:01](https://youtu.be/SSmJKnUOsAc?t=6481)).

**In his big picture:** Judas is the "betrayal" step of the Passion template and a test case for free will against providence.

**In his words:** "He's calm He's reassured He's already forgiven Judas Escariat" [Civ 41 · 30:10](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=1810)

### Simon Peter (Saint Peter)

*Who:* Leading apostle of Jesus; Catholic tradition holds he was the first bishop of Rome, martyred there in the 60s CE.

**What he teaches**
- At the arrest Peter cuts off a servant's ear; Jesus sends him away and submits ([Civ 47 · 31:13](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1873)). Afterwards Peter "will become the first bishop of Rome. Basically the first pope" ([Dante 2 · 2:01:02](https://youtu.be/eF5B3CWpKxg?t=7262)).
- In Paradise Peter examines Dante on faith; Dante answers that God is love instead of reciting the Nicene Creed, which Jiang calls heresy ([Dante 4 · 43:36](https://youtu.be/brRNCLD7to0?t=2616)). It's ironic, he says, since in the Gospels Peter "has the least faith in Jesus" ([Dante 5 · 14:14](https://youtu.be/xPxEi5Zm0G4?t=854)).
- Peter's outburst in Paradise 27 against the one who "usurps my place" means Dante is calling the pope the Antichrist ([Dante 4 · 1:50:36](https://youtu.be/brRNCLD7to0?t=6636)).
- Dante saw Peter's becoming pope, and the apostles' fight over Jesus's legacy, as "perverting Jesus' legacy" ([Dante 5 · 12:17](https://youtu.be/xPxEi5Zm0G4?t=737)).

**In his big picture:** Peter personifies the papacy's claim to authority, which Jiang, through Dante, sets against Jesus's anti-hierarchical message. (In Civ 26 he calls Constantine the first pope.)

**In his words:** "This is clearly what Peter's saying is that the Pope is the Antichrist." [Dante 4 · 1:56:04](https://youtu.be/brRNCLD7to0?t=6964)

**Reality check:** Most historians think Rome had no single ruling bishop until the second century; "Peter the first pope" is a later tradition.

### James the Just

*Who:* Brother of Jesus and leader of his followers in Jerusalem; Josephus says the high priest Ananus had him stoned around 62 CE.

**What he teaches**
- James inherited Jesus's movement, called "the poor" or Ebionites ([SH 22 · 07:57](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=477)). That he stayed safely in Jerusalem is Jiang's key evidence the Jews did not kill Jesus ([Civ 25 · 12:00](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=720), [SH 22 · 18:17](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1097)).
- He clashed with Paul over circumcision and Jewish law; in Jiang's account Paul tried to bribe him and claimed his vision outranked James's kinship ([Civ 25 · 18:31](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=1111), [Civ 25 · 20:08](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=1208)).
- The war of 66–73 wiped out James's group and left Paul the authority ([Civ 26 · 11:34](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=694)); in 2025 he goes further: "It was Paul who had him killed" ([SH 23 · 1:32:48](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=5568)).
- The Ebionites later went to Arabia to "help found the religion of Islam" ([Civ 25 · 11:11](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=671)).

**In his big picture:** James stands for the "real" Jesus movement (poor, Jewish, egalitarian) that Paul's church displaced and that flows, in his account, into Islam.

**In his words:** "And James does is like, of course, he's not God. He's my brother man." [SH 23 · 31:02](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=1862)

**Reality check:** Josephus blames the high priest, not Paul, for James's death; nothing suggests Paul arranged it (his letters do mention a collection of money for Jerusalem). An Ebionite root for Islam is speculative.

### Paul of Tarsus

*Who:* Diaspora Jew and former Pharisee (died c. 64–67 CE) who became Christianity's leading missionary to non-Jews; his letters are the oldest writings in the New Testament.

**What he teaches**
- **The real founder.** A third of the New Testament is by or about Paul, so Christianity is "a religion about Jesus" ([Civ 25 · 10:01](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=601)). He swapped good works for faith in Jesus, built hierarchy, and dropped circumcision for converts ([Civ 25 · 15:24](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=924), [Civ 25 · 17:30](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=1050)).
- **The spy theory.** Why would a rich, educated Roman citizen join illiterate peasants, rarely quote Jesus, and fund long journeys with secretaries? "he's clearly a spy" ([SH 23 · 23:21](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=1401)). In 2024 he called this an idea "I've never heard before but which I do believe" ([Civ 25 · 36:46](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=2206)); by 2025 Paul might equally be working for the Jewish leadership, or both ([SH 23 · 1:12:44](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=4364)).
- **Defusing Jewish fanaticism.** Rome feared Jewish zeal, built on purity, a persecution complex and a coming Messiah of war. Paul answered each: drop circumcision, become Roman citizens (citizenship saved Paul where Jesus died), and accept a Messiah of peace who has already come ([Civ 25 · 40:56](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=2456), [Civ 25 · 45:52](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=2752)).
- **Franchiser and salesman.** Paul was Ray Kroc to Jesus's McDonald brothers, except "Jesus hated hamburgers" ([Civ 25 · 33:40](https://youtu.be/kuMhSFXnr5I?t=2020)). His letters are "sophistry" that redefine love as obedience, with a pitch of "50% off today" ([SH 23 · 1:08:05](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=4085), [SH 23 · 1:16:21](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=4581)).
- **A church for masters.** Paul copied the Roman patriarchal household and the Dionysian mysteries (a virgin-born god killed, resurrected and ritually eaten, hence the Eucharist). Patriarchs converted with their slaves, who now worked for heaven ([SH 23 · 08:21](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=501), [SH 23 · 13:51](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=831)).
- **A system that lasts.** In SH 23 the Church lets Rome's families store their power as a "black nobility" of "13 families from Rome that still run the world today", allied with Jews through secret societies, and Jews had to "agree to be scapegoats" to survive (see Chapter 10) ([SH 23 · 1:27:45](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=5265), [SH 23 · 1:32:04](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=5524)).

**In his big picture:** Paul is his archetype of the organizer who turns a prophet into an empire; in his joke, Satan's answer to religion is simply to organize it ([SH 23 · 1:13:01](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=4381)).

**In his words:** "Jesus is a prophet, but Paul is a business person." [SH 23 · 19:14](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=1154)

**Reality check:** No ancient source suggests Paul was an agent of Rome or of Jewish leaders; he does quote Jesus's Last Supper words (1 Corinthians 11, read in SH 23). The "13 families" ruling the world is a conspiracy theory without evidence, and casting persecuted Jews as having agreed to it echoes antisemitic tropes.

### The Gnostics (Sophia and the Demiurge)

*Who:* Modern label for religious movements of the 2nd–3rd centuries CE that taught salvation through secret knowledge (gnosis); texts found at Nag Hammadi describe a flawed creator, the Demiurge, born from the fallen divine being Sophia.

**What he teaches**
- **The myth.** The perfect Monad emanates pairs of beings; Sophia ("knowledge") creates alone, without her partner or the Monad's permission, and produces a monster, which she hides. Ignorant of anything higher, it thinks it is God and makes our world: "in the Bible the monster's name is Yahweh" ([Civ 24 · 43:45](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2625), [Civ 24 · 45:31](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2731)).
- Hence evil and suffering; to win the monster's game you must become a monster, which is why the rich are evil and the poor blessed ([Civ 24 · 46:31](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2791)).
- In Secret History he retells the myth (the "demi urge") as the secret guarded by secret societies, who also hold that the richest people are Nephilim ([SH 5 · 38:59](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2339), [SH 5 · 36:34](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2194)). A variant he credits to Dante: the Monad allowed our flawed world so our mistakes would expand its imagination ([SH 5 · 46:29](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2789)).
- Paul's church displaced them, and he calls the medieval Cathars gnostics too ([Civ 26 · 08:42](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=522), [Civ 40 · 46:14](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=2774)).
- By August 2026 he distances himself: he doesn't accept the demiurge or Satan, seeing the demiurge as a patch for the Bible's contradictions ([Meet-Up 2 · 1:13:11](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=4391)).

**In his big picture:** Gnosticism supplies his vocabulary (monad, divine spark, false world) and his picture of Jesus, the Cathars and Dante as one suppressed tradition.

**In his words:** "in each of us there's a Divine spark of the monad and so we can defy or escape this reality created by a monster" [Civ 24 · 47:23](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2843)

**Reality check:** Texts like the *Apocryphon of John* do tell this Sophia-and-Demiurge myth, but they postdate Jesus; the Nephilim claims have no evidence behind them.

### Constantine the Great

*Who:* Roman emperor (306–337) who converted to Christianity, legalized and favoured it, convened the Council of Nicaea (325) and founded Constantinople (330).

**What he teaches**
- Great kings consolidate power with new religions (David, Augustus); after a civil war Constantine adopted Christianity "to unify the Empire", and "he was the first pope" ([Civ 26 · 17:42](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1062)).
- Paul's small church won because the war of 66–73 destroyed the Temple and James's group, and because its hierarchy mirrored the Roman patron-client household, so aristocrats became bishops and brought their networks ([Civ 26 · 10:47](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=647), [Civ 26 · 15:23](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=923)).
- **Nicaea.** Rejecting modalism, partialism and Arianism, the bishops chose the Trinity: "an equation", not a story, which no one can enter, debate or combine, only obey ([Civ 26 · 24:50](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1490)). It makes "God is nothing and everything", so symbols become reality: money, science, the nation-state ([Civ 26 · 31:17](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1877)). Later he calls it a "bureaucratic invention" meant to create mystery and distance ([Civ 33 · 56:27](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=3387)).
- **Constantinople.** Scholars cite strategy; Jiang says culture. A consultant can't reform an entrenched school and must start a new one, so Constantine moved east to "switch cultures", from pagan, Latin and republican to Christian, Greek and bureaucratic ([Civ 33 · 28:29](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=1709)).

**In his big picture:** Constantine is where Paul's church meets imperial power, and his Trinity is Jiang's starting point for the modern "monotheistic" mind.

**In his words:** "great Kings always want to introduce new religions to consolidate their Authority" [Civ 26 · 17:42](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1062)

**Reality check:** Constantine did not make Christianity the state religion (Theodosius did in 380, as Jiang says in [Civ 27 · 04:42](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=282), though SH 24 credits Constantine), and he was never pope. The creed makes Father and Son co-equal, not "not equal" as Civ 26 says; the Spirit's place was defined in 381.

### Augustine of Hippo

*Who:* North African bishop and theologian (354–430), author of *Confessions* and *The City of God*, the most influential Latin Church Father.

**What he teaches**
- "the intellectual architect of the Catholic church" ([Civ 27 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=0)). After Rome's sack in 410 many blamed Christianity, "a religion of slaves"; *City of God* split Rome, the earthly city of pride, from Jerusalem, the city of God ([Civ 27 · 05:46](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=346)). Standing outside history, the Church could bless whichever king won ([Civ 27 · 08:34](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=514)).
- **Radical original sin.** The stolen pears in *Confessions* retell Eden: we sin for pleasure. Paul saw the Fall as a mistake; for Augustine "we are the mistake", made out of nothing, "a failed science experiment" ([Civ 27 · 15:52](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=952), [Civ 27 · 36:11](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=2171)).
- **Obedience over action.** Curiosity and love lead to sin (Adam fell because he loved Eve, as Virgil's Dido was ruined by love) ([Civ 27 · 33:26](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=2006)). Jiang's reductio: don't save a drowning boy, since "doing nothing is the best thing you can do" ([Civ 27 · 23:39](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=1419)). Augustine's verdict on Lucretia's suicide is "gaslighting" ([Civ 27 · 21:45](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=1305)).
- His books are a "rhetorical manual" for priests; his rise to bishop at 42 suggests a powerful family, and his output a team of scribes ([Civ 27 · 27:44](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=1664), [Civ 27 · 47:38](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=2858)).
- **Legacy.** The Dark Ages, the blueprint for Charlemagne's empire, communism's "sacrifice now, paradise later" logic ([Civ 27 · 43:04](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=2584)), and in 2025 the teaching that the divine spark "is from Satan" ([SH 23 · 1:17:28](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=4648)).

**In his big picture:** Augustine is the villain of his medieval story, the champion of obedience whom Dante, champion of free will, must refute ([Civ 29 · 14:20](https://youtu.be/GfqJi0qevok?t=860)).

**In his words:** "what atin [Augustine] does is he takes the Catholic church out of History" [Civ 27 · 06:39](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=399)

**Reality check:** Augustine never advised against saving a drowning child; that is Jiang's extrapolation. His authorship is not doubted (he dictated to stenographers), and most historians now avoid the "Dark Ages" label.

### Justinian I

*Who:* Byzantine emperor (527–565) who reconquered Italy and North Africa, codified Roman law and built the Hagia Sophia.

**What he teaches**
- Byzantium peaked under Justinian and his general Belisarius, then paid with overextension and the "Justinian plague" ([Civ 33 · 12:51](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=771), [Civ 33 · 13:48](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=828)).
- **Emperor versus general.** Though friends (their wives too), Justinian sabotaged Belisarius and tried him for treason, fearing a conqueror would take his throne; afterwards Byzantium turned defensive and bribed its enemies ([Civ 33 · 15:32](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=932)).
- He expanded to "enforce the Holy Trinity on all churches", driving dissenting Christians into Persia and Arabia ([SH 24 · 41:39](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=2499), [SH 24 · 41:54](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=2514)).
- In Dante's Paradise 6 he narrates "the official chronology as presented by the Catholic Church", which Dante quietly subverts ([Dante 2 · 1:39:13](https://youtu.be/eF5B3CWpKxg?t=5953)).

**In his big picture:** Justinian is imperial orthodoxy at full strength, and the reason the heretics who later rallied to Muhammad were in the desert.

**In his words:** "So, Justinian goes on this holy crusade to conquer and unite all the Roman Empire and enforce the Holy Trinity on everyone and he succeeds." [Dante 2 · 2:35:58](https://youtu.be/eF5B3CWpKxg?t=9358)

### Muhammad

*Who:* Arab prophet (c. 570–632) from Mecca and founder of Islam; Muslims believe the Quran records the revelations he received.

**What he teaches**
- We know little: the Quran barely mentions him, and an early source is a Christian (Armenian) bishop writing 20–30 years after his death about a merchant-preacher claiming Abraham's promised land for Ishmael's descendants ([Civ 28 · 01:06](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=66)). Tradition adds Gabriel in the cave, the flight to Medina and the Constitution of Medina promising religious freedom ([Civ 28 · 10:14](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=614)).
- **Why the Arabs won.** Arabia was "the hotbed of innovation": cosmopolitan traders, warriors trained as imperial mercenaries, and heretics (anti-Trinitarian Christians, Jews, Zoroastrians) fleeing persecution, while Byzantium and Persia were exhausted by war, plague and succession fights ([Civ 28 · 21:29](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=1289), [Civ 28 · 28:24](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=1704)).
- **Three messages** united them: all are Abraham's children, so there is religious freedom; merit over class and tribe; and a jihad to reclaim the promised land, from the Nile to the Tigris, from empires and landlords ([Civ 28 · 36:38](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=2198)). He compares it to the Taiping Rebellion, Brazil's Canudos war and the French Revolution ([Civ 28 · 14:19](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=859)).
- **Erased history.** The revolution became an empire and whitewashed its origins (as China does with Mao), purging the companions ([Civ 28 · 43:23](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=2603), [Civ 37 · 45:30](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2730)). He named no successor because he expected the end of days ([Civ 37 · 48:47](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2927)).
- **Theology.** Jesus is a messenger, not God, and the Trinity is nonsense, a relief to persecuted Christians; Islam is "really the first monistic religion in the modern sense" ([Civ 37 · 37:04](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2224), [Civ 37 · 39:45](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2385)). By 2025: "Mohamad himself did not preach Islam", only that he was God's messenger promising tolerance ([SH 24 · 32:12](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=1932)).
- Dante put him among the schismatics in Hell because Catholics saw Islam as a heresy splitting from Christianity ([Dante 8 · 2:48:50](https://youtu.be/oFK53cYz_h0?t=10130)).

**In his big picture:** Muhammad is his model of a prophet whose revolution against empire succeeded, then was domesticated by the empire it created.

**In his words:** "Muhammad was a revolutionary who wanted to overthrow the social order in order to establish the Kingdom of Heaven" [Civ 28 · 38:42](https://youtu.be/C9rzPGJ0wm4?t=2322)

**Reality check:** The Constitution of Medina survives in early biographies, not the Quran (Civ 37 places it there). His suggestion that al-Aqsa was first meant as a Jewish Third Temple, which he calls "extremely controversial", has no documentary support ([Civ 37 · 50:31](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=3031)).

### Charlemagne

*Who:* King of the Franks (768–814), crowned emperor in Rome by Pope Leo III on Christmas Day 800.

**What he teaches**
- The coronation was "the first time that the pope could anoint a monarch" ([Civ 34 · 01:03](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=63)). He credits Charlemagne with armoured knights, the Carolingian Renaissance and cathedrals like Aachen, built so the priest's voice felt like God's ([Civ 34 · 02:09](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=129), [Civ 34 · 04:01](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=241)).
- **Why take a crown from the pope?** Europe's mountains, forests and lack of great rivers made unity by conquest impossible, so rule needed legitimacy, unity and differentiation from Byzantium ([Civ 34 · 09:57](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=597), [Civ 34 · 47:11](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=2831)). Leo III wanted to assert Rome against Constantinople and needed protection after nearly being assassinated ([Civ 34 · 48:25](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=2905)).
- **Ideas move history.** Charlemagne had *City of God* read to him daily, and the empire realized Augustine's blueprint ([Civ 34 · 54:26](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=3266)). Voltaire was right that it was neither holy, Roman nor an empire ([Civ 34 · 56:18](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=3378)).

**In his big picture:** Charlemagne shows legitimacy, not force, holding fragmented Europe together, with the Church as kingmaker.

**In his words:** "the king and the pope are benefit from pretending the Catholic church is in charge in order to confer legitimacy onto the king" [Civ 34 · 56:18](https://youtu.be/1dwvgtc7XzY?t=3378)

**Reality check:** Einhard confirms Charlemagne's love of *City of God*; election of emperors by prince-electors, which Jiang describes, was formalized much later (1356).

### Pope Urban II

*Who:* Pope from 1088 to 1099 who launched the First Crusade at Clermont in 1095.

**What he teaches**
- Around 1000 the Church faced a legitimacy crisis: Muslims held Jerusalem, Muslim Spain was richer, the 1054 schism split East and West, corruption was rampant, and people wanted God without a middleman ([Civ 40 · 16:39](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=999)).
- "popes are politicians": asked by Byzantium for help against the Seljuks, Urban saw a chance to unite Christendom under Rome and turn peasant religious energy outward ([Civ 40 · 26:12](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1572)).
- **Reading the speech.** Instant remission of sins for those who die fighting is a call to jihad ([Civ 40 · 27:05](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1625)); "despised and base race which worships demons" is racism built on fake atrocity rumours ([Civ 40 · 28:00](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1680)).
- Crusaders' motives (penance, immunity, vengeance, landless younger sons, the Second Coming, chivalry) later drove the conquest of the Americas ([Civ 40 · 33:48](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=2028)), and "the crusading mentality" persists in today's Middle East wars ([Civ 40 · 58:54](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=3534)).

**In his big picture:** Urban shows crusading as one of the Church's three tools, alongside scapegoating and persecution, for keeping its monopoly.

**In his words:** "this is a jihad he's calling for jihad" [Civ 40 · 27:05](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1625)

**Reality check:** The speech survives only in later chroniclers' versions; the demon-worship line comes from Robert the Monk.

### Francis of Assisi

*Who:* Italian friar (c. 1181–1226), son of a rich cloth merchant, who renounced wealth and founded the Franciscan order.

**What he teaches**
- Born rich, he rejected his family to "marry poverty"; in Dante's Paradise 11 Poverty is a bride whose first husband was Jesus ([Dante 3 · 55:28](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=3328)), echoing Jesus's movement, "the poor" ([Dante 3 · 44:33](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=2673)). His preaching to the sultan makes him a model of faith, hope and love ([Dante 3 · 1:12:59](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=4379)).
- **Real significance.** The "filthy rich" Church sponsored the Franciscans to absorb the poverty movements while keeping them obedient to the pope ([Dante 3 · 1:20:12](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=4812), [Civ 40 · 49:00](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=2940)).
- **Why the order decayed.** The rich gave friars property to share their holiness ("It's a bribe, guys"), so the order grew rich without trying ([Dante 3 · 1:17:36](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=4656)); real poverty also means "social death", which ego and fear resist ([Dante 3 · 1:22:22](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=4942)).

**In his big picture:** Francis is the purest attempt to live Jesus's teaching inside the Church, and proof of how institutions absorb holiness.

**In his words:** "So the Franciscans were a useful political pawn for the church to maintain legitimacy." [Dante 3 · 1:20:12](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=4812)

**Reality check:** Francis founded his order on his own initiative (approved c. 1209–10), before the crusade against the Cathars. SH 24 wrongly makes him leader of "the Dominicans" ([SH 24 · 1:17:35](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=4655)).

### Dominic de Guzmán (Saint Dominic)

*Who:* Castilian priest (1170–1221) who founded the Order of Preachers (Dominicans) in 1216 after years preaching against the Cathars in southern France.

**What he teaches**
- In Paradise 12 a Franciscan praises Dominic, as a Dominican praised Francis: Francis married poverty, Dominic married faith, the Church's "two princes" ([Dante 3 · 1:28:27](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=5307), [Dante 3 · 50:51](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=3051)).
- The order was created for the Cathars, who didn't fear death and whom Catholic neighbours protected; crusading failed, so the Dominicans used persuasion and interrogation ([Dante 3 · 1:30:26](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=5426)).
- **Method.** Educated friars stayed for months, questioned people repeatedly, wrote everything down, caught inconsistencies, isolated the few Cathars and burned the unrepentant to intimidate the rest ([Civ 40 · 1:02:43](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=3763)); he says this medieval inquisition used "very little violence" because doctrine forbade the Church to spill blood ([Civ 40 · 51:31](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=3091)).
- Forerunners of the Jesuits, learning in the service of orthodoxy, they were corrupted by power over life and death ([Dante 3 · 43:54](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=2634), [Dante 3 · 1:38:21](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=5901)).

**In his big picture:** Dominic stands for the Church's intellectual arm: knowledge used to enforce orthodoxy.

**In his words:** "So rather than use the sword, they use the pen." [Dante 3 · 1:33:55](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=5635)

**Reality check:** Dominic died before the papal inquisition was organized in the 1230s, though Dominicans later staffed it; "very little violence" understates the burnings it led to.

### Thomas Aquinas

*Who:* Italian Dominican theologian (1225–1274) whose *Summa Theologiae* fused Aristotle's philosophy with Christian doctrine.

**What he teaches**
- Second only to Augustine among Catholic theologians, he brought Aristotle back into Christian thought, as the Islamic world had already done ([Dante 3 · 42:54](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=2574)).
- The contrast with Islam forced the Church to admit Augustine's problems; one reform was the university, and at Paris Aquinas tried to marry faith and reason ([Civ 41 · 48:58](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=2938)). He was "much more enlightened much more open-minded" than Augustine ([Civ 30 · 56:09](https://youtu.be/asbKjezwIJM?t=3369)).
- He failed because philosophy is argued over while poetry sinks into the subconscious, and Augustine was "in the air you breathe" ([Civ 30 · 57:05](https://youtu.be/asbKjezwIJM?t=3425), [Civ 41 · 49:54](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=2994)).
- In Dante this Dominican praises Francis and admits his own order was corrupted by power ([Dante 3 · 1:24:14](https://youtu.be/6tzAyCZ4Omk?t=5054)).

**In his big picture:** Aquinas is the reformer-philosopher who prepares the ground; Dante the poet finishes the job.

**In his words:** "He's trying to combine faith and reason" [Civ 41 · 48:58](https://youtu.be/v6CDqJnrvLk?t=2938)

**Reality check:** Aquinas's synthesis later became official Catholic theology (endorsed by Leo XIII in 1879), so "he doesn't succeed" judges only his impact in his own era.

## Key ideas from these lectures

- **Gnosticism** (captioned "narcissism"): a perfect Monad above a false, flawed creation, escaped through knowledge; he calls it Jesus's real religion [Civ 24 · 38:20](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2300).
- **The divine spark**: a piece of the Monad in every soul that glows with love and forgiveness and dims with evil; Jesus's message is that it can free even a slave [SH 22 · 28:13](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=1693).
- **Three layers of teaching**: great teachers have public, inner and secret layers that say different but coherent things [Civ 24 · 39:20](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=2360).
- **A religion about Jesus, not by Jesus**: Paul's "trick" of shifting the focus from Jesus's words to Jesus's person [SH 23 · 05:12](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=312).
- **Apocalyptic eschatology**: the belief that God will soon return to destroy his enemies, which drove John, Jesus, the Jewish revolts and Muhammad's followers [Civ 24 · 30:48](https://youtu.be/_bXHBT5NTfc?t=1848).
- **Pater familias / franchise model**: Rome as a platform of patriarchal families with slaves and clients; Paul's church copied it, which is why it spread [SH 23 · 10:46](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=646).
- **The godhead and monotheism**: the Trinity as an unarguable equation making "God is nothing and everything", root of money, science and the nation-state [Civ 26 · 29:58](https://youtu.be/za30rbC3tTg?t=1798). (Later he calls Islam the first monotheism "in the modern sense" [Civ 37 · 39:45](https://youtu.be/2OdO8LoKuo8?t=2385).)
- **Pagan versus Christian worldview**: Christianity brought Rome "truth evil and the individual", replacing chance, heroic action and community [Civ 33 · 34:19](https://youtu.be/abs6z7VPEMc?t=2059).
- **Out of history**: Augustine's move placing the Church above the rise and fall of kingdoms, as the permanent source of legitimacy [Civ 27 · 06:39](https://youtu.be/GdbZBVUIVDE?t=399).
- **Ransom theory**: we became Satan's slaves at the Fall, and Jesus's death bought us back, so we are now God's (the Church's) slaves; Jiang says Dante rejected it [SH 22 · 1:08:57](https://youtu.be/pp0E1gb80WQ?t=4137).
- **The Eucharist as possession**: eating Christ's body dissolves the individual into the Church: "It's possession by Jesus" [SH 23 · 57:02](https://youtu.be/6XGAc_OqCbU?t=3422).
- **Scapegoating, persecuting, crusading**: the medieval Church's three tools for defending its faith monopoly; in scapegoating, Jews working for nobles absorbed peasant anger [Civ 40 · 19:34](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1174), [Civ 40 · 20:31](https://youtu.be/3751ZjwmrBw?t=1231).
- **Messenger versus organizer**: a prophet says "I'm a messenger"; later a Paul-like figure turns his words into an empire, as with Zoroaster, Jesus and Muhammad [SH 24 · 33:19](https://youtu.be/TEdVhyE8pO0?t=1999).
- **Christ consciousness**: the Second Coming understood as Jesus entering a person who truly forgives [GB 1 · 26:35](https://youtu.be/TsD-8FGA84A?t=1595).
