# Chapter 9: Revolutions, Philosophers and the Modern Mind

*Mainly drawn from Civilization #46–48, #50–59 and #END (April–June 2025) and Geo-Strategy Update #6 (July 2025), plus later Secret History, Game Theory and Dante sessions where he returns to these figures.*

## The story he tells

For Jiang, the French Revolution is "the most significant event in human history" [Civ 46 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=0). It is the moment Europe swapped Christianity's faith, orthodoxy and truth for the modern, deist creed of reason, debate and progress [Civ 46 · 04:17](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=257). His thesis is that this secular revolution was "a religious crusade" that needed three kinds of genius: a poet to dream the new world (Rousseau), a prophet willing to die for it (Robespierre) and a prince to conquer it (Napoleon) [Civ 46 · 10:24](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=624). The same cast returns in Russia, with Marx as poet, Lenin as prophet and Stalin as conqueror [Civ 59 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/QtqfIjK1GAc?t=1440).

After 1815, he says, four civilizations fought for the world, each claiming to be the true heir of Rome and each with its own Christianity. Britain was practical and utilitarian and was founded culturally by Shakespeare. America was built as an Enlightenment "game". Russia had a "dark imagination", voiced by Dostoevsky, and Germany had the "will to power" [Civ 51 · 00:00](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=0). Alongside them runs a relay of four thinkers who "fundamentally changed the way we see ourselves". Kant made the world subjective, Hegel gave it a spirit, Marx made it class struggle and Freud turned us inward [Civ 56 · 38:33](https://youtu.be/m24LnQ-Ksjk?t=2313). The course ends with nationalism as a new religion, the modernist "cult of the self" and Stalin as Nietzsche's overman. Jiang later hands that last role to Putin (see Chapter 12).

Three convictions tie these lectures together. The first is that myths, not economics, drive history: when authority collapses, "mythologies are the subconscious operating system of society" and leaders become actors in a play [Civ 47 · 24:30](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1470). The second is that reason without love is dangerous. The third is that the greatest minds, "Homer, Dante, Emanuel Kant", all teach one secret: "The imagination is the animating force of the universe" [Civ END · 1:05:44](https://youtu.be/_gH4PvIni5E?t=3944). Keep the dates in mind. By 2026 he recasts Caesar, Napoleon, Hitler and Stalin as "agents" whom secret societies sponsor [GT 26 · 11:46](https://youtu.be/RG1clZlrfOo?t=706) (see Chapter 10).

## The main figures

### Jean-Jacques Rousseau

*Who:* Geneva-born philosopher and writer (1712–1778) whose *Discourse on Inequality*, *Emile* and *The Social Contract* shaped the French Revolution and Romanticism.

**What he teaches**
- Rousseau is the Revolution's "poet", the first to imagine that a whole new world could be built on reason alone [Civ 46 · 11:28](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=688).
- *Emile* invented modern childhood. Before age 12 a child's reason isn't ready, so let children play. A child left free like a "savage" becomes a problem-solver, while a drilled "peasant" becomes a creature of habit [Civ 46 · 1:07:23](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4043). Jiang agrees: schools exist "to control children", and he may keep his own sons out until 12 or even 16 [Civ 46 · 1:05:00](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=3900), [Civ 46 · 1:09:02](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4142).
- The *Discourse on Inequality* traces inequality, patriarchy and war back to the first man who fenced off land and said "this is mine" [Civ 46 · 1:10:40](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4240).
- *The Social Contract* rests on the general will: what we would all agree on if we each reasoned about the common good. Its opposite is the particular will of a group, such as Beijing wanting road money that the poorest province needs more [Civ 46 · 1:13:05](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4385). This idealism divides the French Revolution from the American one, which assumes people act on particular interests [Civ 46 · 1:14:41](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4481).
- Rousseau's "Count measure compare" (good government can be measured) is "a really dumb idea", but it gave France the metric system [Civ 46 · 1:16:58](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4618). His civil religion of reason underlies French secularism and today's clashes with Muslim immigrants [Civ 46 · 1:20:18](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4818).
- Jiang sets him against Locke. Rousseau believes we are born good and able to reason, and that law should follow the general will, not tradition. His line leads to communism and Nazism, while Locke's leads to the US Constitution [Civ 50 · 44:09](https://youtu.be/laGSxfvlOfk?t=2649).

**In his big picture:** Rousseau supplies the dream and the vocabulary of the first nation-state. Even Lincoln's "of the people, by the people, for the people" is, in Jiang's reading, Rousseau restated [Civ 58 · 18:16](https://youtu.be/tquo1UsC5Nw?t=1096).

**In his words:** "The social contract is the bible of the French revolution" [Civ 46 · 1:10:40](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=4240)

### Maximilien Robespierre

*Who:* Provincial lawyer from Arras (1758–1794) who led the Jacobins and the Committee of Public Safety during the Reign of Terror and was guillotined in July 1794.

**What he teaches**
- Robespierre is "one of the most misunderstood and underrated individuals in human history" [Civ 46 · 12:24](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=744). He was a nerdish provincial lawyer and a disciple of Rousseau. He worked 18 hours a day, gave over 500 speeches, defended the poor, and arrived in Paris and left it with no money [Civ 47 · 15:30](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=930).
- The Terror was human sacrifice, as among the Aztecs, Vikings and Romans. It stirred blood lust and terrified enemies. Above all, killing the king broke a taboo, so there could be no going back [Civ 47 · 19:13](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1153), [Civ 47 · 20:10](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1210). Jiang reads out Robespierre's doctrine of "virtue and terror" [Civ 47 · 1:08:54](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=4134).
- He sided with the poor and attacked the Declaration of the Rights of Man for making property sacred, a vision Jiang calls communism [Civ 47 · 53:16](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=3196), [Civ 47 · 1:05:28](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=3928).
- Jiang offers what he calls an argument no one has made before. Robespierre could have been king but chose to act out the Passion of Jesus. He was betrayed by allies, withdrew into isolation, refused to rally his followers, was shot in the jaw and was jeered on the way to the guillotine. He became scapegoat, martyr and role model at once [Civ 47 · 00:59](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=59), [Civ 47 · 32:56](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1976).
- This happened subconsciously: when authority breaks down, the shared myth (here the Christ story) takes over and leaders must play its roles [Civ 47 · 1:20:02](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=4802). His death purified the Terror's "polluted energy" and turned the Revolution into a hurricane for Napoleon to ride [Civ 47 · 1:25:32](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=5132).
- He was the greater man than Napoleon because he promoted talent selflessly. Jiang credits him with the merit-based army Napoleon later used [Civ 48 · 03:07](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=187), [Civ 48 · 20:59](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=1259).

**In his big picture:** Robespierre is the model "prophet", whose self-sacrifice gives a revolution its moral fuel. Lenin plays the same part in Russia [Civ 59 · 25:19](https://youtu.be/QtqfIjK1GAc?t=1519).

**In his words:** "the moment that he died G Rosepier became Jesus he became in the minds of the French people second coming of Jesus" [Civ 47 · 29:14](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1754)

**Reality check:** The Christ thesis is Jiang's own. Jiang puts the Terror's toll at "at least 40,000" in Paris alone. Historians count about 16,600 official death sentences, some 2,600 of them in Paris, and perhaps 35,000–40,000 deaths in all. Whether Robespierre's jaw wound came from a gendarme or a suicide attempt is still debated. The army reforms are usually credited to Lazare Carnot and the mass levy.

### Napoleon Bonaparte

*Who:* Corsican-born general (1769–1821) who seized power in 1799, crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804, dominated Europe and was finally defeated at Waterloo in 1815.

**What he teaches**
- Jiang starts with a trick question: who is rarer, a battlefield genius (Napoleon, Caesar) or a leader who only promotes talent (Robespierre)? The second, because it takes selflessness [Civ 48 · 01:56](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=116). Austerlitz was a reckless gamble saved by his officers, above all Davout, whom many rate the better general [Civ 48 · 12:13](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=733).
- Napoleon "was not a great general" but a political operator and myth-maker who saw himself as Alexander and Caesar reborn [Civ 48 · 31:43](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=1903). He fought in Italy because Caesar came from there, went to Egypt because Alexander and Caesar had, and crossed the Alps because Hannibal did [Civ 48 · 41:09](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=2469), [Civ 48 · 43:29](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=2609).
- His St Helena remark ("i would found a religion", with himself carrying a new Quran) shows he understood the Revolution as a crusade and cast himself as its Messiah [Civ 48 · 33:48](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=2028).
- Crowning himself emperor in 1804 broke the play, because a Messiah may not take a crown [Civ 47 · 1:22:15](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=4935). France became a military cabal that needed endless war to keep his myth alive. His letters to Josephine reveal a narcissist: "power is my mistress" [Civ 48 · 47:15](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=2835).
- His 1806 defeat of Prussia forced rivals to liberalize: they abolished serfdom and opened the civil service to the middle class [Civ 48 · 23:33](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=1413). Britain, he adds, beat Napoleon through the Bank of England's "infinite financing" [Civ END · 06:00](https://youtu.be/_gH4PvIni5E?t=360).
- Figures like him appear at the end of republics: Caesar, Napoleon, Hitler, and perhaps Trump, whose genius is myth-making rather than policy [Civ 48 · 55:48](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=3348).

**In his big picture:** Napoleon is the "prince" of Jiang's revolutionary cycle, the charismatic myth-maker who turns a republic's energy into empire.

**In his words:** "if you can control these mythologies you can control people you can become the emperor" [Civ 48 · 32:23](https://youtu.be/enEET_0j_Y8?t=1943)

**Reality check:** Most military historians rank Napoleon among the greatest commanders in history. Davout was a superb marshal, but calling Napoleon "not a great general" is a minority view.

### John Locke

*Who:* English philosopher (1632–1704) whose *Two Treatises of Government* and *Essay Concerning Human Understanding* made him a founder of liberalism and empiricism.

**What he teaches**
- Hobbes held that any government beats the chaos of nature. Locke answers that we are born with inalienable rights to life, liberty and property, and may overthrow a government that fails to protect them. That makes Locke "the founder of liberalism" and a source of the US Constitution [Civ 50 · 39:25](https://youtu.be/laGSxfvlOfk?t=2365).
- He is Jiang's foil for Rousseau and Kant. For Locke we are born a blank slate, society exists for liberty and tradition grounds law. For Rousseau we are born with reason, society exists to develop it and the general will grounds law [Civ 50 · 40:17](https://youtu.be/laGSxfvlOfk?t=2417).
- Jiang tests the blank slate with a thought experiment: your memory is wiped on a fruitful island. If Locke were right you would starve, putting stones in your mouth. Kant's inborn categories would let you quickly sort the edible from the inedible [Civ 55 · 30:28](https://youtu.be/_3c3FjS57x4?t=1828).
- Jefferson's Declaration "is just copying word for word basically" Locke [Civ 52 · 23:11](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=1391). In 2026 he calls Locke's property rights part of a "money is God" ideology that British finance sponsored [GT 17 · 18:23](https://youtu.be/VbowBTJWKBo?t=1103).

**In his big picture:** Locke anchors the practical, empiricist Anglo-American tradition that Jiang sets against idealist continental thought.

**In his words:** "government is only legitimate if it guarantees us our inelible rights Right So lo is considered the founder of liberalism" [Civ 50 · 39:25](https://youtu.be/laGSxfvlOfk?t=2365)

**Reality check:** Locke's triad was "life, liberty and estate". Jefferson's "pursuit of happiness" draws on Locke but is not a copy. Locke wrote the *Two Treatises* in the 1680s, before the Bank of England existed (1694). Historians link them to the crises that led to the Glorious Revolution, not to financial sponsorship.

### William Shakespeare

*Who:* English playwright and poet (1564–1616), author of about 38 plays including *Hamlet*, *Julius Caesar*, *Othello* and *King Lear*.

**What he teaches**
- Shakespeare founded the British Empire culturally by making English flexible and memorable. English now carries soft power, persuading the young that Anglo-American culture is the best [Civ 51 · 08:01](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=481), [Civ 51 · 09:05](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=545).
- His tool was diction: 1,700–3,500 new uses of words [Civ 51 · 12:53](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=773). Jiang invents examples like "I daggered him with questions" to show how bent words rewire the brain [Civ 51 · 14:46](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=886). Iambic pentameter made the plays near-musical, memorable entertainment for commoners, staged beside bear-baiting pits and brothels [Civ 51 · 18:04](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=1084), [Civ 51 · 21:31](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=1291).
- "To be or not to be" can mean live or die, kill or not kill, obey or defy fate, or ask what existence is. All four readings are right, and that layering is the power [Civ 51 · 28:37](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=1717). Hamlet thinks too much ("paralysis is analysis") and may reflect the death of Shakespeare's son Hamnet [Civ 51 · 23:13](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=1393).
- In *Julius Caesar*, Brutus argues by antithesis (I loved Caesar, but Rome more). Antony answers with chiasmus, collapsing that opposition and turning the crowd [Civ 51 · 34:50](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=2090). *Othello* is about envy of a great achiever, not race [Civ 51 · 1:08:26](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=4106).
- He saw people clearly because he was not elite-educated [Civ 51 · 1:10:58](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=4258). Yet beside Homer and Dante, Jiang wonders whether he is "a pretty nothingness": beautiful language as a reality unto itself, without deep truths [Civ 51 · 52:24](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=3144).
- In 2026 he pictures Dante dining with Shakespeare after *Macbeth*. Dante asks whether Shakespeare is a fatalist, whether he believes in God and what he thinks of women [Dante 10 · 24:37](https://youtu.be/MDOK9qaaA7Q?t=1477).

**In his big picture:** Shakespeare is the third founding poet, after Homer (Greece) and Dante (modernity). His English is a meeting place for the world, but one filtered through a utilitarian, skeptical, empirical outlook [Civ 51 · 49:40](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=2980).

**In his words:** "shakespeare turns English into the world's linguistic internet a platform in which all cultures ideas and worldviews can meet and crossbreed" [Civ 51 · 48:32](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=2912)

### John Milton

*Who:* English poet and polemicist (1608–1674) who defended free speech in *Areopagitica* and, blind, dictated the epic *Paradise Lost* (1667).

**What he teaches**
- In May 2025 he found parts of *Paradise Lost* beautiful but, as a grand vision, "a very limited and narrow-minded epic" next to Homer, Virgil and Dante [Civ 51 · 54:15](https://youtu.be/Qms7trnKwqQ?t=3255).
- By September 2025 it was "the greatest poem in English language". Its author was a rebel and free-speech advocate who, Jiang says, belonged to secret societies, and the poem is their "foundational text" [SH 5 · 44:08](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2648), [SH 5 · 49:22](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=2962). By December, Milton was "definitely my favorite poet" [SH END · 1:26:39](https://youtu.be/WFWizN3QoPg?t=5199).
- He reads Satan as the hero. Volunteering alone to escape hell is heroic [SH 5 · 54:18](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=3258). His speech to Eve argues that a true God would reward risk and knowledge. Yale taught Jiang that Satan lies, but Genesis, he says, shows the serpent told the truth [SH 5 · 1:00:37](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=3637).
- The poem defines Anglo-American culture: pride is good because it brings us nearer godhood and so drives progress. Augustine and Dostoevsky call the same pride evil [GT 23 · 27:02](https://youtu.be/6aNh6sBpqvQ?t=1622), [GT 23 · 37:29](https://youtu.be/6aNh6sBpqvQ?t=2249).

**In his big picture:** Milton's Satan becomes his emblem of Western striving and transgression (for Satan generally see Chapter 2, for secret societies Chapter 10).

**In his words:** "When you actually read the Bible, Satan is telling the truth." [SH 5 · 1:00:37](https://youtu.be/EgGfQdv_rPc?t=3637)

**Reality check:** Relaying a critique from his former Yale teacher David Bromwich, Jiang conceded he offers "a very minority interpretation of Paradise Lost" [GT 24 · 03:07](https://youtu.be/8nsxuB3Vsts?t=187). There is no evidence that Milton belonged to a secret society.

### Alexander Hamilton

*Who:* Caribbean-born American founder (1755/57–1804), Washington's aide, co-author of *The Federalist Papers* and the first Treasury Secretary.

**What he teaches**
- Hamilton, not Washington, is "really the founder of America". He had a vision of empire, built on a strong central government and industry [Civ 52 · 24:58](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=1498), [Civ 52 · 25:57](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=1557).
- His clash with Jefferson set agrarian democracy, individual rights and slave agriculture against empire, industry and free labor. That clash is the root of the Civil War, and the North's victory made Hamilton's vision real [Civ 52 · 30:10](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=1810), [Civ 52 · 40:14](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2414).
- The Federalist Papers show the Constitution is "risk management". It aims not at greatness but at avoiding anarchy, civil war, disunion and tyranny. Senate confirmation, for example, lets senators embarrass a president who appoints his idiot son [Civ 52 · 35:19](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2119), [Civ 52 · 36:47](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2207).
- In 2026 he rejects a viewer's suggestion that Hamilton, Franklin and Lincoln were British "assets" [GT 29 · 11:58](https://youtu.be/RE2UribEFIo?t=718).

**In his big picture:** Lincoln fuses Hamilton's empire with Jefferson's democracy into the "empire of democracy", and its decline is where the course ends.

**In his words:** "the genius of American revolution is actually a Hamilton he is the really the founder of America" [Civ 52 · 24:58](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=1498)

### Abraham Lincoln

*Who:* 16th US president (1861–1865), who led the Union through the Civil War, issued the Emancipation Proclamation and was assassinated in 1865.

**What he teaches**
- He is ranked the greatest president because, after America's bloodiest war, he gave the slaughter a meaning at Gettysburg [Civ 52 · 40:58](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2458), [Civ 52 · 41:52](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2512).
- The Gettysburg Address resolves Hamilton versus Jefferson: America becomes a democracy with an imperial mission to spread liberty. The Pax Americana later fulfilled that mission [Civ 52 · 44:54](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2694), [Civ 52 · 46:47](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2807).
- The war "was not about slavery" but about states' rights, democracy against empire [Civ 52 · 40:14](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2414). He argued the same in 2024 [GS 11 · 10:16](https://youtu.be/Go1bMQKnJBQ?t=616).
- Tsar Alexander II admired Lincoln. He sent warships to New York and San Francisco to deter Britain and France from backing the Confederacy [Civ 59 · 05:21](https://youtu.be/QtqfIjK1GAc?t=321).

**In his big picture:** Lincoln turns the American "game" into a crusading faith, the imperial democracy whose overreach Jiang says is now ending.

**In his words:** "what Lincoln does which is revolution is say no this is not a conflict because we are an empire of democracy" [Civ 52 · 44:54](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=2694)

**Reality check:** Most historians see slavery as the central cause of the Civil War, and the seceding states' own declarations say so. The 1863 Russian fleet visits were also a precaution in Russia's own disputes with Britain and France, not only a gesture of support.

### Fyodor Dostoevsky

*Who:* Russian novelist (1821–1881), author of *Crime and Punishment* and *The Brothers Karamazov*, who survived a mock execution and years of Siberian hard labor.

**What he teaches**
- Dostoevsky and Tolstoy are Russia's "two great prophets". Their novels try to reconcile a civilization torn between Europe and Asia, Christianity and paganism, Enlightenment and brutal empire [Civ 53 · 34:23](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=2063). Facing a firing squad and then Siberia made him think deeply about life's meaning [Civ 53 · 41:17](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=2477).
- *Crime and Punishment* shows reason's limits. Raskolnikov reasons his way to murder to prove he is a Napoleon, not a Robespierre [Civ 53 · 50:09](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=3009). His heart rebels, and he is saved only by surrendering to Sonia's love, just as Priam's kiss saves Achilles [Civ 53 · 56:36](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=3396).
- The drunkard Marmeladov's speech shows the Orthodox God, who forgives everyone. In Jiang's contrast, Protestants choose to sin, Catholics are born in sin, and the Orthodox are born to sin and can be redeemed only by God [Civ 53 · 46:52](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=2812).
- In the Grand Inquisitor tale, the Church argues that people want authority, not freedom. Jesus answers only with a kiss, because "we can't change someone's reason ever but we can change his heart" [Civ 53 · 1:02:29](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=3749).
- This explains Ukraine. Putin invaded "to save Russian civilization", which values the "mystery miracle and authority" of the heart over America's materialism [Civ 53 · 01:56](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=116), [Civ 53 · 1:03:23](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=3803). In a clash of civilizations "there can only be one winner" [Civ 53 · 1:05:51](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=3951).

**In his big picture:** Dostoevsky is Russia's answer to the Enlightenment. Truth lies in the heart, and reason alone can justify killing millions [Civ 53 · 1:07:32](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=4052).

**In his words:** "what crime and punishment will show us is reason is nothing it doesn't really matter what matters is the heart" [Civ 53 · 42:53](https://youtu.be/0rALqcPWuH0?t=2573)

### Friedrich Nietzsche

*Who:* German philosopher (1844–1900), author of *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* and *On the Genealogy of Morals*, known for the "will to power", the Übermensch and "God is dead".

**What he teaches**
- Nietzsche corrects Schopenhauer. If the world is will and desire, don't deny it like a monk. The will manifests in bodies so that we can expand and impose our will on reality: the "will to power" [Civ 54 · 43:06](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2586). God made us, in effect, to become gods [Civ 54 · 43:57](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2637).
- The Übermensch (superman, overman) ignores public opinion and steps outside history to bend it. Nietzsche's model is Napoleon [Civ 54 · 44:55](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2695).
- The *Genealogy*'s "tamed" animal becomes Jiang's picture of modern life. Society, and school with its grades, is a zoo that turns us into obedient caged animals [Civ 54 · 48:50](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2930).
- Put together, Schopenhauer, Wagner and Nietzsche mean rejecting Christianity for paganism and unity of will. That mood let Hitler promise "to be the next Napoleon" [Civ 54 · 45:47](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2747), [Civ 54 · 49:36](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=2976).
- Later he names Kant, Hegel and Nietzsche as Germany's three great philosophers. He calls Nietzsche Zoroaster come back, for whom "God is creativity" [SH 18 · 34:47](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2087), [SH 18 · 35:47](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=2147), [SH 18 · 59:35](https://youtu.be/dELEdxh0ceg?t=3575).

**In his big picture:** The overman is one of his main lenses on power. Napoleon, Hitler, Stalin and possibly Putin are men who warp history by will [GSU 6 · 33:43](https://youtu.be/ZgvAHZqaawA?t=2023).

## Key ideas from these lectures

- **Poet, prophet, prince**: every successful revolution needs a poet who imagines the new world, a prophet who sacrifices himself to lead people into it, and a prince or messiah who conquers it. In France these were Rousseau, Robespierre and Napoleon; in Russia, Marx, Lenin and Stalin [Civ 46 · 11:28](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=688), [Civ 59 · 24:00](https://youtu.be/QtqfIjK1GAc?t=1440).
- **Mythology as operating system**: when priests and kings lose authority, the shared myth takes over. History becomes a play whose actors must speak their lines; break the role, as Napoleon did by crowning himself, and "the entire play dies" [Civ 47 · 1:21:20](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=4880), [Civ 47 · 1:22:15](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=4935).
- **Breaking the taboo**: public killing, like the Terror's guillotine, is human sacrifice. It unites the crowd, terrifies enemies and burns every bridge back to the old order [Civ 47 · 20:10](https://youtu.be/ef-Ch2LGDDI?t=1210).
- **Reason, debate, progress**: the modern creed, which replaced faith, orthodoxy and truth. Without love to restrain it, reason "can reason anything including concentration camps nuclear bombs genocide" [Civ 46 · 05:17](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=317), [Civ 46 · 30:52](https://youtu.be/5HwZx5kQm9o?t=1852).
- **General will**: Rousseau's idea of what everyone would choose by reasoning about the common good, not by voting their appetites. In Jiang's example, the general will picks salad for lunch, not ice cream [Civ 55 · 26:14](https://youtu.be/_3c3FjS57x4?t=1574).
- **Unity of will**: the pagan, German ideal of a people acting as one behind a leader, against Christian individual free will. It is the source of fascism's power and of what he calls synchronicity in war [Civ 54 · 51:10](https://youtu.be/o2Nq--qU9Kc?t=3070).
- **Übermensch (overman)**: a figure who steps outside history and warps it to his will, such as Napoleon, Hitler, Stalin and perhaps Putin [Civ 59 · 01:49](https://youtu.be/QtqfIjK1GAc?t=109).
- **Geist ("the gist")**: Hegel's world-spirit, which Jiang pictures as an internet we all plug into. He later equates it with Jung's collective unconscious and with God [GSU 6 · 36:55](https://youtu.be/ZgvAHZqaawA?t=2215).
- **Cult of the self**: his definition of modernism, from Freud and Jung through Joyce and Woolf to social media. It is an inward-turned individualism that isolates people and blocks collective action [Civ 57 · 21:40](https://youtu.be/cylkQPsfFRY?t=1300).
- **Transgression**: one answer to the Protestant "crisis of faith" is to prove your faith by breaking social taboos, as in his shoplifting thought experiment [Civ 57 · 07:38](https://youtu.be/cylkQPsfFRY?t=458), [Civ 57 · 08:43](https://youtu.be/cylkQPsfFRY?t=523).
- **America as a game**: the founders built not a civilization but a fair, winnable money-making game open to all immigrants. It is an "anti-civilization" whose winners eventually take everything [Civ 52 · 1:01:25](https://youtu.be/ea20aMYfT5I?t=3685).
- **Nationalism as religion**: of all the answers to the crisis of faith (money, reason, the self), "God is in the nation" proved the most powerful, because it also cures alienation [Civ 58 · 04:55](https://youtu.be/tquo1UsC5Nw?t=295).
